Short answer: Claude for Financial Advisors is a Cowork plugin Anthropic launched on September 14, 2026, that connects Claude to custodians, asset managers, and wealth-tech platforms — Charles Schwab, BlackRock, Addepar, Envestnet, and others — so advisors can research, prep meetings, and draft compliance documents without switching tools. It ships now to Enterprise customers through the Cowork plugin browser.

## What is Claude for Financial Advisors, exactly?

Short answer: it's a plugin that bundles data connectors to wealth-management platforms with eight prebuilt workflow skills, letting an advisor ask Claude to pull account data, draft a client briefing, or check a recommendation against firm policy inside one conversation. It is not a new model or a standalone app — it runs inside Cowork, Anthropic's agentic workspace product.

That distinction matters for buyers. A firm doesn't purchase "Claude for Financial Advisors" as its own subscription; it enables the plugin for advisors who already have Cowork access, then connects the custodians and platforms the firm actually uses. Anthropic frames it as an extension of Claude's existing financial-services push rather than a separate product line.

## Which platforms and workflow skills does it connect to?

Short answer: the plugin covers custodians and asset managers (Schwab, BlackRock, Vanguard), wealth-tech platforms (Addepar, Envestnet, iCapital, Orion, SS&C Black Diamond, Wealthbox, Wealth.com, Zocks), and previously available connectors (Microsoft 365, Salesforce, DocuSign, Box, FactSet, S&P Global, Morningstar). The plugin ships with eight prebuilt workflow skills covering the advisor's actual weekly routine.

| Layer | What's included |
|---|---|
| Custodians & asset managers | Charles Schwab, BlackRock, Vanguard |
| Wealth-tech platforms | Addepar, Envestnet, iCapital, Orion, SS&C Black Diamond, Wealthbox, Wealth.com, Zocks |
| Pre-existing connectors | Microsoft 365, Salesforce, DocuSign, Box, FactSet, S&P Global, Morningstar |
| Workflow skills | Advisor onboarding, alternative investments briefing, compliance and AI policy review, estate and tax briefing, portfolio rebalance review, post-meeting notes and follow-up, pre-meeting prep, prospect intake |

Eight named skills is a deliberately narrow scope — Anthropic isn't asking Claude to freelance across an advisor's entire job, it's targeting the specific documents and reviews that eat the most prep time. The pre-meeting prep skill is a good example of that focus: instead of asking Claude to give investment advice, it asks Claude to assemble account history, recent transactions, and open action items into one briefing before a client call — a repetitive task that eats hours a week but doesn't require real judgment to assemble.

## How does it compare to OpenAI's ChatGPT for Financial Services?

Short answer: the two land five days apart but target different buyers — OpenAI's ChatGPT for Financial Services, announced September 9, 2026, is built for investment bankers and equity researchers inside ChatGPT Work, while Claude for Financial Advisors targets wealth advisors managing individual client portfolios inside Cowork.

| | Claude for Financial Advisors | ChatGPT for Financial Services |
|---|---|---|
| Launched | September 14, 2026 | September 9, 2026 |
| Primary user | Wealth advisors | Investment bankers, equity researchers |
| Host product | Cowork plugin | ChatGPT Work |
| Core use case | Client-facing prep, compliance drafts | Research notes, financial models |

The one-week gap is close enough that it reads as a direct response rather than a coincidence — both companies are racing to lock in wealth-management and banking customers before the other one does.

## Who can actually use it, and what does it cost?

Short answer: as of September 2026, Claude for Financial Advisors is limited to Enterprise customers who access it through the Cowork plugin browser — there's no self-serve or individual-plan version yet. Anthropic hasn't published a separate price for the plugin itself; it rides on top of an existing Claude Enterprise and Cowork contract.

That makes it a tool for firms that have already bought into Anthropic's enterprise stack, not something a solo advisor or a small three-person RIA can turn on this week. If your firm doesn't already run Cowork, the practical first step is talking to your Anthropic account team about Enterprise access rather than looking for a public sign-up page.

That narrow scope is probably deliberate rather than accidental: wealth management is one of the more heavily regulated sectors a consumer-facing AI product can touch, and rolling it out broadly without an enterprise-gated pilot would create unnecessary exposure for Anthropic and its customers alike. The enterprise-first approach buys firms time to onboard connectors alongside their own compliance teams instead of individual advisors turning them on unsupervised.

## What compliance and data risks should a firm check before turning this on?

Short answer: verify which connectors have read-only versus write access, confirm where client data is processed and stored under your data-residency requirements, and treat every Claude-drafted document as something a human must sign off on before it reaches a client. The "compliance and AI policy review" skill helps draft policy language, but it doesn't replace your firm's actual compliance sign-off process.

This is the same tension every regulated-industry AI rollout hits: connecting Claude to Schwab or BlackRock account data means that data now flows through a new system, and your firm's existing retention and audit-log rules need to explicitly cover it. Firms already using Anthropic's Managed Agents advisor and data-residency controls have a head start here, since those settings extend to plugins like this one.

My honest take: the connector list is the easy part — any vendor can sign partnership agreements. The real test is whether an advisor trusts the portfolio-rebalance skill's numbers enough to hand a client a document without re-checking every line, and Anthropic hasn't published an accuracy benchmark for that yet. Until one exists, treat this as a very capable drafting assistant, not a source of truth. The five-day gap after OpenAI's competing launch doesn't look coincidental either — both companies are racing to lock in the same regulated, high-budget customer segment, and as that race speeds up, the pressure to ship more connectors tends to outpace the pressure to publish accuracy benchmarks.
