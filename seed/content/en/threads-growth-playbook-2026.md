Short answer: yes, Threads passed X in mobile daily active users as of January 2026, so it is no longer a platform you can afford to treat as an afterthought. Growth there does not come from chasing likes — it comes from starting reply chains, posting on a steady cadence, and leaning on text and images instead of links. The feed rewards conversation, not clicks.

## Did Threads really overtake X in 2026?

Yes, on one specific metric: mobile daily active users (DAU). [Similarweb's independent measurement](https://www.phonearena.com/news/if-youre-still-on-x-youre-becoming-minority-at-least-on-mobile_id177449) put Threads at roughly 141.5 million mobile DAU in January 2026, versus roughly 125 million for X in the same period. Meta's own figure, given on its late-2025 earnings call, was closer to 150 million DAU — the gap comes down to measurement methodology, not a contradiction. On monthly actives, Meta announced 400 million-plus in August 2025, and third-party estimates put that number near 450 million in early 2026. The takeaway: [Threads is now a real alternative pulling mobile traffic away from X](https://gulfnews.com/technology/media/threads-just-beat-x-on-daily-mobile-users-new-data-shows-1.500412791), not a side account people check once a week. Part of that shift traces back to a maturing desktop web version, topic-follow expanding discovery beyond your own following list, and users leaving X's pricing and moderation debates for a default mobile app that just works.

## What does the Threads feed actually reward?

Replies, not likes. The number of replies a post gets, and how long that reply thread keeps running, outweighs raw like counts in what gets surfaced — so a post that invites disagreement or a follow-up question outperforms one that only states a fact. Topic follows add a second discovery layer: when someone follows a specific topic, posts tagged under it reach people outside your existing followers. A single-sentence claim followed by your own reply saying "here's why I think that" consistently out-performs the same claim written as one long paragraph, because the algorithm reads a growing thread under your own post as a distinct engagement signal. The practical result: [how often you post](/en/posts/how-often-should-you-post-2026) matters less than whether you keep replying to your own posts as the conversation develops.

## What posting cadence and format actually work on Threads?

One to three original posts a day, paired with consistently replying to others, beats posting ten times in one burst and going quiet for a week. Three formats hold up most reliably: short text hooks where the first line has to work with zero context, quote posts that add your own take to someone else's post, and mini-series added as replies to your own earlier post. The same logic behind [the first lines that stop the scroll](/en/posts/first-lines-that-stop-the-scroll) applies here — if the opening sentence doesn't promise something on its own, nothing after it gets read.

## Why do posts with links get less reach?

Threads has historically distributed posts containing an outbound link with lower reach than link-free posts; Meta hasn't published an exact penalty rate, but the pattern creators report is consistent — the same content posted without a link, with the link added as the first reply instead, reaches more people than the same post with the link in the caption. The practical rule is to write the main post so it stands on its own without the link, then drop the URL into the first reply to your own post. Rather than expecting Threads alone to carry traffic, it works better as one discovery channel alongside [Threads, Bluesky, and X](/en/posts/threads-vs-bluesky-vs-x-2026) rather than a replacement for either.

## Is Threads' API or a scheduler worth using?

Yes, but only for consistency, not for discovery. Threads' public API and the third-party schedulers built on it let you queue posts in advance and publish them automatically at a set time — a practical way to keep hitting that 1–3 posts a day target during travel or a busy week. What scheduling does not automate is replying: the actual growth driver is answering replies live in the 30–60 minutes right after you post, and a post that misses that window rarely recovers the same conversation momentum no matter how well it was timed. Scheduling solves when to post; who you talk to afterward is still on you.

## How do you cross-post from Instagram without it looking lazy?

Posting the same image with the same caption on both platforms underperforms. What works better is publishing the Instagram post first, then writing a Threads post that explains the decision or the debatable part behind that same image rather than re-describing what's already visible in it — same visual, different reason for existing. The same logic behind [what to post with zero audience](/en/posts/what-to-post-zero-audience-2026) applies: content posted without a platform-specific reason to exist loses reach on both platforms, not just one.

## What should you actually measure for Threads growth?

Track average replies per post, the share of replies that convert into a profile visit, and how much discovery comes from topic follows rather than your existing audience — not impression counts. A post that gets few impressions but a lot of replies is a stronger growth signal to repeat than one with high impressions and silence underneath it. My take: accounts that use Threads as a mirror of their Instagram feed don't grow; accounts that write for Threads' own logic — conversation, pushback, a reply thread that keeps extending — do.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Threads' mobile daily active user count as of January 2026?

Similarweb's independent measurement put Threads at roughly 141.5 million mobile daily active users in January 2026, ahead of X's roughly 125 million in the same period. Meta's own figure, cited around its late-2025 earnings call, was closer to 150 million DAU.

### How does a brand-new Threads account start growing?

Pick 5–10 topics to follow first, then post 1–3 original posts a day in those topics while consistently replying to other people's posts. Growth comes from the number of replies a post gets and the conversation that follows, not from the like count.

### Why do links get less reach on Threads?

Threads has historically distributed posts containing an outbound link with lower reach than link-free ones. The practical fix is writing the main post so it stands on its own without a link, then adding the URL as the first reply to that same post.

### Can you repost Instagram content directly to Threads?

Technically yes, but the same image with the same caption gets weak engagement. A better result comes from using the same image but writing a Threads-specific caption that explains the decision or debate behind the post instead of repeating what the image already shows.
