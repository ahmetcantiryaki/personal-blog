Short answer: based on 2026 data, 2–5 posts a week on Instagram, 2–5 a week on LinkedIn, and 1–4 a day on TikTok are reasonable ranges. No platform publishes an official "this number is correct" threshold, though — the data we do have points to consistent rhythm mattering more than the raw count.

## What's a realistic posting frequency per platform?

Vendor data across the three platforms (estimates compiled by scheduling tools like Buffer, Social Champ, and Hootsuite from their own user data — not official platform statements) gives close but not identical ranges. Buffer's 2026 ["State of Social Media Engagement" report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement/), which looked at over 52 million posts, found that top-performing accounts post both more often *and* more consistently than the median account; the report itself states there's no single magic number that works across all platforms, niches, and account sizes.

| Platform | Common advice range | Source note |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 2–5 per week | Social media manager survey + Buffer's 2.1M-post analysis |
| LinkedIn | 2–5 per week | Voketa data: returns diminish past 5 posts/week |
| TikTok | 1–4 per day | Industry guides; TT Calculator's 1.9M-video analysis finds peak per-post engagement at once a day |

## Does frequency or consistency matter more?

The data points to consistency. [TT Calculator's analysis of 1.9 million TikTok videos](https://ttcalculator.net/data/engagement/posting-frequency-engagement-correlation/) found per-post engagement peaks at once a day (4.52%), while posting four or more times a day raises total weekly reach but lowers engagement per individual post — meaning "post more often" isn't universally better, it's a tradeoff that depends on whether your goal is total reach or per-post quality. It's also worth noting the accounts in that sample ranged from 8.8 million to 162 million followers, so the numbers don't transfer directly to a small account.

## How should frequency change with account size?

A large account (hundreds of thousands of followers) doesn't benefit from the same frequency advice as a brand-new one. A large account gets a baseline floor of views on every post simply because of its existing follower base, even at high volume; a new account instead needs to signal to the algorithm that "this account produces regularly," which can justify posting slightly more often in the first months (say, 1–2 times a day on TikTok). Accounts that taper frequency down toward quality as their follower count grows generally keep growing without losing engagement rate.

LinkedIn data is actually contradictory across sources: one report says 3–5 posts a week drives 2–3x more total engagement, while a separate secondary source claims engagement rate (per impression) peaks at just 2 posts a week. This contradiction comes down to what's being measured — "total engagement" and "per-post engagement rate" answer different questions: one measures volume, the other measures quality.

## How do you actually set a posting schedule?

Before picking a fixed number, answer three questions: is your goal total reach or a loyal audience (reach favors more frequency, loyalty favors a more consistent rhythm); how many people are producing the content (a one-person team can't sustain four TikTok videos a day); and what cadence can you sustain without your current content quality dropping. The answers turn into something like this:

```text
Weekly Posting Plan — Example
Monday: LinkedIn (long-form post)
Wednesday: Instagram Reels + TikTok (same clip, adapted per platform)
Friday: LinkedIn (short opinion post)
Weekend: Instagram carousel (weekly recap)
```

## How does adapting content across platforms affect frequency?

Cutting separate versions of a single shoot for Instagram, TikTok, and LinkedIn is the cheapest way to raise your frequency without producing fresh content from scratch for each platform. Cropping a product-demo video vertically for TikTok, repurposing the same clip in square format for Instagram, and turning its text summary into a LinkedIn post can fill a three-platform posting schedule from a single production session. The one risk with this approach is ignoring each platform's own hook and format norms while adapting — posting the exact same unedited video in three places performs worse than versions adapted per platform.

## How do you handle cadence during a vacation or crunch period?

For stretches when you can't maintain your regular rhythm — a vacation, a heavy project week, being sick — the better fix isn't dropping to zero, it's pre-producing content and keeping scheduled posts running. Batching three or four pieces of content a week ahead and loading them into a scheduler loses less reach than skipping the week entirely, because algorithms weigh that steady rhythm more heavily than whether the account is actively online at that moment. Not being able to reply to comments in real time on a scheduled post is a minor downside, but it costs less than going fully silent.

## What's the sign you're posting too much?

If your per-post engagement rate is dropping but your follower count isn't growing, that's usually a sign volume has outpaced quality. Likewise, if your production process has become unsustainable — scrambling for an idea every single day — cutting frequency and focusing on quality usually performs better. Our [creator burnout guide](/en/posts/creator-burnout-sustainable-content) covers how to recognize that line before you cross it.

My own take: "how often should I post" is usually the wrong question. The right one is "can I sustain this cadence for six months without the quality dropping?" An account that posts three videos a day for one week and then goes silent for three weeks performs worse than one that posts twice a week and never skips — because both algorithms and viewers penalize irregularity, not the absolute number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many times a week should I post on Instagram?

Vendor data suggests 2–5 posts a week is a reasonable range; most social media managers surveyed work within that range, and Buffer's 2.1M-post analysis found a strong growth correlation at that cadence. This isn't an official Instagram statement — it's an observed estimate.

### Do I need to post more than once a day on TikTok?

No. TT Calculator's analysis found the highest per-post engagement at one post a day; posting 4+ times a day raises total weekly reach but lowers engagement per post. Decide based on whether you're optimizing for volume or quality.

### What happens if I can't post consistently?

An irregular rhythm (posting heavily one week, then nothing for several) performs worse than a steady, lower-volume rhythm; Buffer's 2026 report found top accounts post both more often and more consistently — if you have to pick one, consistency comes first.

### Should I post more than 5 times a week on LinkedIn?

It's not generally recommended; one data source shows diminishing returns past 5 posts a week. Posting more just fatigues the same follower base more often with less impact per post.
