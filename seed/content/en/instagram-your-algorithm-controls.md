Short answer: Instagram's "Your Algorithm" controls are now live across Feed, Reels, and Explore — you can manually pick your interests, and the platform reads keywords in your captions as a search signal that Google and Bing also index. Using it well means posting original content, writing captions that match real search intent, and tracking your save rate instead of your like count.

## What is Instagram's "Your Algorithm" feature?

Your Algorithm is a control panel where users can directly shape their feed with preferences like "show me less of this" or "show me more of this." It started on Reels in December 2025, expanded to Explore in April 2026, and is now live globally across all of Instagram, including the main Feed.

That means the algorithm now weighs explicit stated preference, not just past behavior like watch time and likes. The practical result for a brand or creator: niche targeting is now more predictable, because a viewer can directly say "show me more of this topic."

## What does search-first indexing mean?

Public Instagram reels are now indexed by Google and Bing, which means content with the right keywords, alt text, and subtitles in its caption can pull in search traffic from outside the app entirely. This shift is why keyword-rich captions now outperform hashtag-heavy strategies.

In practice, this means writing a caption like "How to make a flat white at home: a 3-minute method" instead of "best coffee tips today #coffee #foryou." Your caption now gets read the way a search engine reads a page title, not the way a hashtag list gets scanned.

| Old approach | 2026 approach |
|---|---|
| Hashtag stacking (#reels #fyp #viral) | Search-intent captions written as natural sentences |
| Recommendations based only on past behavior | User-selected "Your Algorithm" preferences |
| Like-driven measurement | Save rate and sends-per-reach driven measurement |
| Reposted/aggregator content | Original content prioritized |

## When did "Your Algorithm" roll out, and which surfaces does it cover?

The feature arrived in stages: tested on Reels in December 2025, expanded to Explore in April 2026, and now live globally across every Instagram surface, including the main Feed. That staged rollout shows Instagram testing the feature on a narrow surface first, watching the behavioral data, then expanding once it confirmed stated preference didn't conflict with behavioral signals.

The practical takeaway for a brand or creator: if you're still only tracking performance on Reels, you now need to account for the same preference mechanism shaping distribution on the main Feed too. Seeing different performance across the two surfaces is usually a sign that your audience's interest preferences on that surface have settled differently.

## What ranking signals changed for Reels?

The core Reels ranking order is: watch time and completion rate first, then sends per reach, then likes per reach. This ordering measures how genuinely watchable content is more reliably than a like count — a like is a passive signal, while a send or a save shows the viewer actually valued the content.

That shows up concretely in a real pattern: a creator posting at an average 40% completion rate switched to search-query-style captions and opened each video with a clear question in the first three seconds. Completion rate climbed to 58%, and even with the same raw view count, reach roughly doubled — because the algorithm reads a high completion rate as a signal to distribute content more broadly.

## How do you write a keyword-driven caption?

A three-step process works: first, identify a question or phrase your audience would actually search for ("how to," "which is better," and similar search patterns); second, put that phrase at the start of your caption, since Instagram and search engines both weight the opening more heavily; third, don't skip subtitles and alt text — spoken words in your video get indexed too.

```text
Weak caption: "Tried this today 😍 #foryou #viral #trend"
Strong caption: "Why does chicken breast dry out in an air fryer? 3 mistakes"
```

The second caption matches a real search query and tells the viewer what the video covers upfront, which makes it easier for them to decide to watch to completion.

## Why does original content outrank reposts now?

Instagram tuned its 2026 algorithm to push aggregator content — reposted material pulled from other platforms — behind original production. That means accounts uploading watermarked TikTok clips or compilations pulled from other creators see their reach narrow.

The practical takeaway: instead of uploading a clip from another platform as-is, reshoot it, write a new caption, or produce a locally distinct edit. ## What does "Your Algorithm" change for brand accounts?

The most concrete shift for a brand account is that your audience now uses an active filter instead of a passive one. When a viewer picks "less fashion content," the ad spend or organic effort aimed at that viewer is largely wasted going forward, because the platform takes that preference seriously. That makes audience segmentation more critical than before — instead of producing generic content for a broad audience, you need to understand which sub-audience is choosing "more" of which theme.

A practical method is testing different content themes separately and tracking which one pulls a higher save rate and sends-per-reach. If one theme clearly underperforms the others, that drop might not be a content-quality problem at all — it could reflect viewers actively choosing "less" of that theme.

## How do you measure this? Which metric matters?

Track three metrics instead of your like count: save rate, sends per reach, and average watch time. Instagram Insights' audience retention graph shows exactly which second viewers drop off — that data tells you directly where to tighten the edit on your next video.

Our take: the real value of "Your Algorithm" controls is for viewers, not creators. Leaning on it as a brand and assuming "the viewer already picked their interest" so you can relax on content quality is a risk — a hand-picked interest doesn't make up for a low completion rate. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do you find Instagram's "Your Algorithm" settings?

Open Settings and look for "Your Algorithm" or the "Accounts You Follow and Suggestions" section, where you can choose to see "less" or "more" of specific topics. As of 2026 the feature is live globally across Feed, Reels, and Explore.

### Does Instagram work like a search engine now?

Partly — public Reels are indexed by Google and Bing, so keywords in captions, alt text, and subtitles affect outside search traffic too. This is why hashtag-heavy strategies have given way to natural-sentence captions matching search intent.

### Which signal matters most for Reels ranking?

Ranking looks at watch time and completion rate first, then sends per reach, then likes per reach. Completion rate and sends are treated as more reliable quality signals than likes.

### Does Instagram penalize reposted content?

Not with a direct penalty, but Instagram tuned its algorithm to prioritize original production over aggregator content, which means accounts uploading unmodified clips from other platforms see their reach narrow.
