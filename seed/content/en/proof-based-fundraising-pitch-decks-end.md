Short answer: AI tools now let even tiny teams show a working product, real usage data, and early revenue within weeks, so investors expect it — and a slide deck making the same claims falls to second place once demonstrable proof exists. This doesn't mean the deck disappears; it means the deck can no longer substitute for proof.

## Do investors now want more than a deck?

Yes. A report that reviewed pitch decks from roughly 60 AI startups that raised successfully in 2025 found the traction section now usually comes before the problem and solution slides — the ordering itself shows investors look for "does this work" before anything else. The idea itself is rarely the point of debate anymore; the debate centers on how validated it is.

## What proof do investors expect at each stage?

The type of proof expected shifts as the stage advances:

| Stage | Expected proof | Typical ARR band (AI startups) |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed | Small-cohort retention, early user signal | $100K-$400K |
| Seed | Recurring revenue, growth curve | Usually under $1M, growth rate matters more |
| Series A (AI) | Net revenue retention, expansion revenue | $2M-$4M (baseline reference: $3M) |

Even at pre-seed, "just an idea" is no longer enough — investors expect a measurable retention signal from a small cohort of users. That forces even early-stage teams to ship an MVP fast and start collecting real usage data.

## Where do round sizes and valuations stand in 2026?

The data shows two trends at once: fewer but larger rounds, and a clear valuation premium for AI startups. In the US, the median seed round is $3.0 million (75th percentile $6.0M, 90th percentile $10.0M), and median seed pre-money valuation is $18.4 million. At Series A, median valuation for AI foundation-model startups has climbed to $300 million, versus $55 million for non-AI startups — roughly a 5.5x gap.

## Why are fewer, larger rounds getting funded?

The data points to a clear split: seed funding totals grew 31% year over year while seed deal count fell 30% — the increase comes from larger individual rounds, not more companies getting funded. AI deal counts are at their lowest since 2018, yet average deal size has more than doubled to $24 million. That means capital is concentrating in fewer companies with stronger proof; raising a round as an "average" company has gotten harder, while round size for a company with strong proof keeps growing.

## How do you build a proof-based data room?

Four sections are enough: live product access (not a demo link — a real account), core usage metrics (active users, transaction volume, API call count — whatever you actually measure), a revenue and retention table (monthly recurring revenue, net revenue retention, churn), and customer references (2–3 named customers, ideally a short video or written quote). Keeping these four sections current is a far better use of time than redesigning the deck before every investor meeting; our [guide to building a SaaS financial model with ChatGPT](/en/posts/build-a-saas-financial-model-with-chatgpt) is a practical starting point for the revenue and retention table.

## How do you show proof without falling into vanity metrics?

The most common mistake is leading with a metric that shows growth but doesn't translate into revenue or retention — total sign-ups, app downloads, or "number of companies expressing interest" all fall into this category. Real proof has three traits: it's repeatable (measured month over month, not once), its causal link is explicit (you can clearly explain how this metric turns into revenue), and it's cohort-based (do new users perform as well as old ones, or is early signal inflated). Saying "net revenue retention for the January cohort is 85% at month 3" is far more credible than saying "we grew 20% this month" with the same underlying data.

## How should you present proof in an investor meeting?

Showing proof live during the meeting, instead of summarizing it on a slide, builds investor trust much faster: opening the product on screen and walking through a real transaction together, sharing the actual dashboard behind a metric instead of a screenshot, or playing a short pre-recorded reference call with a customer. What these three methods share is letting the investor answer "should I trust these numbers" through their own observation, instead of taking a slide's claim at face value. The same principle applies to your follow-up email after the meeting: send a link to the data room and updated numbers, not a new slide deck.

## Does this pattern work the same way in every industry?

No. The proof-based approach is especially strong in software and SaaS, where usage and revenue data can be measured digitally and instantly. In hardware, biotech, or regulated sectors (drug development, financial infrastructure), the product development cycle is inherently longer, so a "proof within weeks" standard isn't realistic — investors in these sectors still weight team track record, patent status, and regulatory progress more heavily. Knowing which category your company falls into determines which type of proof to prioritize.

## Is the pitch deck completely unnecessary now?

No. The deck still does three jobs: framing the market-size and timing narrative, showing why the team is suited to solve this problem, and giving context to the proof (which segment, which geography, which business model). But the deck shouldn't outrank the proof anymore — my honest take is that spending 70% of your prep time strengthening proof and the data room, and 30% on the deck, is the opposite of the old ratio, and it's now the right one in the 2026 funding environment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do investors want proof instead of a pitch deck now?

Because AI tools let even small teams show a working product and real usage data within weeks. Once proof is demonstrable, a deck's claims alone are no longer enough for an investor.

### What proof is expected at the pre-seed stage?

A measurable retention signal from a small cohort of users, or early user signal, is now expected; the typical ARR band is $100K-$400K. An idea or prototype alone is no longer considered sufficient even at pre-seed.

### How are Series A valuations for AI startups doing in 2026?

Median Series A valuation for AI foundation-model startups has reached $300 million, versus $55 million for non-AI startups. At the same time, AI deal counts are at their lowest since 2018, while average deal size has climbed to $24 million.

### Is the pitch deck completely unnecessary now?

No, but its role has changed. The deck is now used to frame market size, timing, and team fit; it's expected to support the proof — live product, revenue, retention — not replace it.
