Short answer: as of the September 2026 Android Drop, you can tell Gemini "remember in Find Hub that I put my passport in the bedroom drawer," optionally attach a photo, and later ask Gemini where you left it. It works for untagged items — no Bluetooth tracker required — and is rolling out to phones on Android 16 and later.

## What does Find Hub's new Remembered feature actually do?

Short answer: it lets Gemini save a text note and an optional photo about where you put something, filed under a new "Remembered" tab inside the Find Hub app, so you can search or ask for it later instead of relying on your own memory. This is separate from Find Hub's existing job of tracking phones, tablets, and Bluetooth tag devices like AirTags' Android equivalent.

The distinction matters: before this update, Find Hub could only locate things with a physical tracker attached. Now Gemini and Find Hub can also keep track of untagged items purely from what you tell it — a passport, a spare key, a specific drawer in a specific room.

## How do you set it up and use it?

Short answer: say a command like "Hey Google, remember in Find Hub that I put my passport in my bedroom drawer" to Gemini, attach a photo if you want one, and the entry appears in the Remembered tab; later, ask "Hey Google, where did I put my passport?" and Gemini surfaces the saved note. No manual app setup beyond updating the Find Hub app.

The feature requires the Find Hub app at version 3.1.732-02 or later and a device running Android 16 or newer. Google is rolling it out gradually as part of the broader September 2026 Android Drop, so it may take a few weeks to reach every eligible phone even after you update the app.

## Is storing location notes with Google a privacy concern?

Short answer: yes, in the same way any Google Assistant memory feature is — you're handing Google a searchable log of where your passport, keys, or other valuables sit inside your home, tied to your account. The trade-off is the same one every "remember this for me" AI feature makes: convenience now, in exchange for another category of personal data living on Google's servers.

Practically, this data sits alongside the location and Assistant history you already share with Google if you use any Gemini features, and it's manageable through the same account-level activity controls. If you're not comfortable with Google holding a list of "where I keep my passport," the honest alternative is a sticky note or a note-taking app that doesn't sync to a cloud account tied to your real identity — slower to search, but the data never leaves your device.

## When does this actually beat a sticky note?

Short answer: it wins for infrequently moved, easy-to-forget items — a passport, a specific tool, a spare car key stashed for a house-sitter — where you'd otherwise be searching drawers for ten minutes; it's overkill for things you touch daily, where muscle memory already does the job. A photo attachment helps most when the item itself is easy to misidentify, like "which of these three cables is the one I meant."

The realistic use case is closer to a seasonal or occasional item — winter gear, a passport between trips, spare keys for a rental property — than a daily-carry object. If you're reaching for something every day, you don't need Gemini to remember where it is; you already do.

## What else shipped in the September 2026 Android Drop?

Short answer: alongside Remembered items, this drop added Motion Assist (a screen overlay that reduces motion sickness in cars, buses, and trains on Android 17+), Google Keep list-sharing inside Google Messages chats, custom Chat Themes, and Guided Vision, which gives Gemini Live audio guidance for accessibility use cases.

Motion Assist can trigger automatically when the phone detects vehicle movement, with a Quick Settings tile as a manual shortcut. None of these features depend on each other — you can use Remembered items without touching Motion Assist — but they all ship as part of the same monthly Android feature rollout rather than a numbered OS update.

My take: this is a genuinely useful, low-stakes feature — the kind of small utility that's easy to underrate next to flashier agentic AI announcements, but it's also a reminder that every "let AI remember it for you" feature quietly grows the pile of personal data tied to your account. Worth using for the occasional-item case; worth knowing it's there before you say something you'd rather not have logged.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I use Gemini's new Remembered feature in Find Hub?

Say a command like "Hey Google, remember in Find Hub that I put my passport in the bedroom drawer," optionally attach a photo, and the note appears in Find Hub's new Remembered tab for you to search or ask about later.

### Do I need a Bluetooth tracker for Find Hub to remember an item?

No. The Remembered feature works for untagged items based purely on the text note (and optional photo) you give Gemini — it doesn't require a physical Bluetooth tracker like Find Hub's existing device-tracking feature does.

### What phone and app version do I need for Remembered items?

You need Android 16 or later and the Find Hub app updated to version 3.1.732-02 or newer. Google is rolling the feature out gradually, so it may not appear immediately even after updating.

### Is it safe to store sensitive item locations with Gemini?

It's the same privacy trade-off as any Google Assistant memory feature — your notes are tied to your Google account and manageable through account activity controls, but if you're not comfortable with that, a private note-taking app or a sticky note keeps the data off Google's servers entirely.
