Short answer: Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash's video-extension feature, added August 27, 2026, generates a new 10-second continuation that picks up from the last frame of a clip you already have. Chain several of these and you can stretch a clip to 40 seconds total. It's available through Google Flow and the Gemini app on AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra subscriptions.

## What is Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash, and how is it different from the original?

When Google shipped the original Gemini Omni Flash model on May 19, 2026, it could only generate standalone 10-second clips — there was no way to extend an existing video. The August 27, 2026 Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash update fills that gap: you can now pick a clip you already have and ask the model to generate a new 10-second segment that continues its motion and style. Each extension is a separate request, so stretching a clip to 40 seconds takes three extension calls on top of the original.

On September 23, 2026, the same extension feature landed inside Google Vids, Google's workplace video editor. The Vids integration raises the resolution of extended segments to 1080p and adds duration control — a level of clarity and control the Flow version didn't have.

## How does clip extension actually work?

You need a clip already generated by, or uploaded into, Gemini Omni. Opening it in Flow and choosing "extend" triggers the model to use the last frame as a reference and generate a continuation that preserves motion, camera angle, and lighting as closely as it can. Adding a directive prompt like "camera pans right, the subject keeps walking" steers the continuation toward what you actually want, instead of letting the model guess.

| Feature | Gemini Omni Flash (May 2026) | Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash (Aug 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Clip generation | 10 seconds, standalone | 10 seconds, standalone |
| Video extension | None | Yes, 10s increments, up to 40s total |
| Access | Flow, Gemini app | Flow, Gemini app, + Google Vids from Sept 23 |
| Resolution (extension) | — | Standard in Flow, 1080p in Vids |

## How would a creator actually use this?

The most practical use is stretching a clip you already shot but that's too short for Reels or Shorts. A product-demo clip you shot at 8 seconds can be extended to 18–28 seconds by having the model continue the same scene — no reshoot needed. Three things matter when doing this: the sharper and more static the original clip, the more consistent the extension turns out; clips with fast camera movement or busy backgrounds are more prone to visual artifacts when extended; and because each extension is a separate generation, drift from the original frame becomes noticeable by the third segment.

```text
Example prompt (for extension):
"Continue the scene: keep the camera static at the same angle,
have the subject keep talking, don't change the background light."
```

As we cover in our [guide to fixing Reels with Gemini](/en/posts/fix-reels-with-gemini-hooks-retention), the first 3 seconds of an extended clip still come from your original footage — extension doesn't change hook quality, it only generates what comes after. Our [guide to turning long videos into Shorts with AI](/en/posts/turn-long-videos-into-shorts-with-ai) covers the opposite job: cutting a long video down into short clips.

## Which kinds of clips extend best?

Three scenarios work best in practice: static-camera product shots (since the camera doesn't move, the model has less to guess when generating the continuation), talking-head clips (consistency stays higher because the face and background don't change), and b-roll of a single continuous motion, like a product rotating in someone's hand. By contrast, fast-cut footage, clips with multiple moving subjects, or busy backgrounds raise the risk of visible inconsistency by the third extension segment. Asking "how many things are moving in this scene" before you extend a clip is a quick way to rule out which footage is a bad candidate.

## What do you do if an extension doesn't come out right?

If the generated segment doesn't match the original scene — a color-tone shift, a distorted face, an object that shouldn't be there — the most practical fix is resending the same request with a more specific prompt: a directive like "same lighting, same angle, just have the subject keep talking" shrinks the model's room for free interpretation. A second approach is evaluating each 10-second segment individually instead of generating all the way to 30 seconds at once, and stopping as soon as you're unhappy with a segment — that wastes less time than generating all three segments and only then being disappointed with the result.

## Does the extension feature have usage limits?

Google differentiates video generation quota across the AI Plus, Pro, and Ultra tiers, with Ultra getting the highest monthly generation allowance and Plus the lowest. Every extension call draws from the same quota as generating a brand-new 10-second clip — so extending one clip three times costs as much quota as four separate video generations (the original plus three extensions). That makes it worth confirming the first 10-second extension actually looks usable before extending a clip all the way out, so you don't burn quota on failed attempts.

## Where else does clip extension get used besides Reels and Shorts?

Beyond content creation, clip extension also shows up in product demos, training videos, and short ad formats. An e-commerce brand can stretch an 8-second product-rotation clip from a photo shoot out to 25–30 seconds, producing multiple ad variations from a single shoot. Teams making training content use extension to preserve the feel of an uninterrupted screen recording — if a live demo recording cuts out partway through, generating a continuation of the existing footage saves time compared to reshooting the lost portion.

## What are the output's limits, and who's responsible for labeling it?

The output doesn't always continue perfectly — small inconsistencies in a face or background detail tend to show up by the third or fourth extension segment. Google embeds an invisible SynthID watermark in content generated by Gemini, which technically marks the content as AI-generated — but that doesn't replace a platform's own visible "AI-generated" labeling requirements, like the mandatory labeling policies Meta and TikTok enforce. Our [guide to AI content labels](/en/posts/ai-content-labels-for-creators) covers exactly when that visible label is required.

My own take: video extension is less contentious than "generate an AI video from nothing" features, because the underlying material — your original footage — is real; the model is only predicting what comes next. Still, creators need to keep the line visible to viewers between using this to patch a shot that ran short and inventing a scene that never happened — otherwise a third of a clip people assume is real footage might be a guess.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long can I extend a video with Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash?

Each extension adds a 10-second segment, and you can extend up to a total of 40 seconds — meaning up to three extension calls on top of your original clip.

### Is video extension free?

No, the feature is available on AI Plus, Pro, or Ultra subscriptions; Gemini's free tier does not include video extension.

### Is extension in Google Vids different from Flow?

Yes. The Vids integration added September 23, 2026 generates extended segments at 1080p resolution and adds duration control; extensions done in Flow don't guarantee that same level of clarity.

### Does the extended video carry a visible AI label?

Google adds an invisible SynthID watermark to the output, which lets the content be technically detected as AI-generated — but you may still need to apply your platform's own visible "AI-generated" label separately, depending on its policy.
