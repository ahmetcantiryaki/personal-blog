## Why your landing page isn't converting

If your landing page underperforms, the fix is rarely "more convincing copy." The average page converts around 2.35%, while the top 10% clear 11.45% or higher, and the gap between those numbers is almost never a paragraph problem. It's a [message-match, speed, and friction problem](https://landerlab.io/blog/landing-page-conversion-rate).

Marketing teams love to argue about headlines. Nobody wants to argue about Largest Contentful Paint. That's backward, and it's costing conversions every single day.

## The "more copy = more trust" myth

There's a persistent belief that if visitors aren't converting, they simply haven't been convinced yet, so the answer is another testimonial, another bullet list, another paragraph explaining the value proposition. In July 2026, the data still doesn't support that instinct. Landing pages that hit "Good" ratings across all three Core Web Vitals convert roughly 35% above the mobile industry average, according to [web.dev's Core Web Vitals documentation](https://web.dev/articles/vitals). No amount of extra copy buys back that gap. Speed, clarity, and friction reduction outperform persuasion volume almost every time. Here's where teams actually lose conversions.

### 1. Headlines that don't match the ad

If someone clicks a search ad for "affordable CRM for small teams" and lands on a headline about "the future of enterprise workflow," you've lost them before they scroll. Message-match failures are one of the most common and most fixable landing page errors: the headline, subheadline, and hero image need to echo the exact language of the campaign that drove the click. This matters even more given where that traffic usually comes from — if you're weighing paid channels, see our comparison of [Google Ads versus Meta Ads on a small budget](/en/posts/google-ads-vs-meta-ads-small-budget) for how audience intent differs by platform.

### 2. Too many competing CTAs

A landing page with a "Sign Up," a "Learn More," a "Download the Guide," and a "Talk to Sales" button isn't offering choice, it's offering decision paralysis. Every additional call to action dilutes the one action you actually need. Contact-page or lead-gen pages should have a single primary CTA repeated at logical scroll points, not four different exits competing for the same click.

### 3. Slow LCP and layout shift quietly bleeding conversions

This is the mistake nobody wants to own because it's an engineering problem, not a copywriting one. Across a dataset of 2.1 million landing page sessions, every additional second of delay in Largest Contentful Paint beyond the 2.5-second threshold costs roughly a 7% drop in conversion rate, per [Digital Applied's 2026 landing page statistics](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/landing-page-statistics-2026-conversion-data-points). Teams will spend three meetings debating whether a headline should say "boost" or "increase," then ship a hero video that pushes LCP past four seconds. That trade-off makes no sense, and it's happening on marketing teams right now.

### 4. Hero sections that hide the actual offer

If a visitor has to scroll past a full-bleed animation, a client-logo carousel, and a mission statement before finding out what you're actually selling, you've buried the offer. The hero section is prime real estate: it should state what the product does, who it's for, and what the visitor should do next, all above the fold.

### 5. Missing or weak social proof

A landing page with no testimonials, no logos, no numbers, and no case study snippet is asking for trust it hasn't earned. This doesn't mean piling on quotes, it means placing one or two credible, specific proof points near the CTA, where the decision is actually being made.

### 6. Forms that ask for more than they need

Every field you add to a form is a small tax on completion, and the data on this is consistent and blunt.

| Form fields | Completion rate |
|---|---|
| 1 (email only) | 13.4% |
| 3 | 10.1% |
| 5 | 7.8% |
| 7 | 5.3% |
| 9 | 3.6% |

Roughly speaking, each additional field costs about 11% of your completions, per [SEO Sherpa's landing page statistics](https://seosherpa.com/landing-page-statistics/). If your "Request a Demo" form asks for job title, company size, phone number, and a dropdown of how you heard about them, you're trading data enrichment for conversions you'll never get back. Collect the minimum to start a conversation, then qualify later.

### 7. Design that isn't mobile-first

Most landing page traffic, especially from paid social and search ads, arrives on mobile. A page designed on a 27-inch monitor and squeezed down to fit a phone screen almost always ships tap targets that are too small, forms that require pinch-zooming, and hero copy that wraps awkwardly. Mobile-first isn't a checkbox, it's the primary design constraint, and Core Web Vitals thresholds are measured against real-world mobile conditions, not your office Wi-Fi.

## The audit checklist

Before you launch, or before you blame the copy for a weak conversion rate, run the page through this:

```text
1. Headline matches the exact ad/search query that drove the click
2. Single primary CTA, repeated at logical scroll points
3. LCP under 2.5s on mobile (throttled 4G test, not office Wi-Fi)
4. INP under 200ms on primary interactions
5. CLS under 0.1, no layout jumps as images/ads load
6. Offer is stated above the fold, no scroll required to understand it
7. At least one specific, credible proof point near the CTA
8. Form asks for the minimum fields needed to start the conversation
9. Mobile layout tested on an actual phone, not a resized browser
10. One clear next step, not three competing exits
```

If your page fails more than two or three of these, that's the conversion leak, not the copy.

## The blunt take

Teams will spend three meetings arguing over whether a CTA button should say "Get Started" or "Start Free Trial," then ship a hero video that tanks LCP by two seconds and quietly costs 14% of conversions. Stop guessing about copy tone and start measuring the things that actually move the number: message match, load speed, and form friction. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's a good landing page conversion rate in 2026?

It depends heavily on vertical. E-commerce typically runs 2–3%, SaaS landing pages 5–7%, general lead-generation pages 3–5%, and high-intent service businesses can reach 8–15%. The overall median across industries sits around 2.35%, per [LanderLab's conversion rate data](https://landerlab.io/blog/landing-page-conversion-rate).

### Does page speed really affect conversions, or just SEO ranking?

Both. Core Web Vitals were built as a ranking signal, but the underlying user experience they measure, load speed and visual stability, directly affects whether visitors stay long enough to convert. Pages hitting "Good" across all three CWV metrics convert about 35% above the mobile industry average.

### How many form fields should a landing page have?

As few as the goal allows. A single-field email capture converts around 13.4%, while a nine-field form drops to roughly 3.6%. If you need more information, collect it in a follow-up step rather than gating the first conversion behind it.

### Should I rewrite my headline or fix my page speed first?

Fix speed first if LCP is over 2.5 seconds. It's a bigger and more measurable lever than most headline tests, and it compounds: a slow page undermines every other change you make on top of it.
