Short answer: ChatGPT's camera update, rolled out around October 1, 2026, lets you capture several pages in a row that auto-combine into one document, tap a Try On button to see clothing on a photo of yourself, and save products you're browsing to a Favorites list — all from the same camera interface you already use to ask ChatGPT about what you're looking at.

## What does the new camera scanning feature actually do?

It captures multiple pages in sequence — receipts, contracts, handwritten notes — and auto-combines them into a single document instead of making you photograph, save, and merge each page yourself. The scanning itself got sharper edge detection and glare handling, so a page photographed at an angle under normal room lighting comes out readable without a manual crop step. This sits inside the same camera mode you'd already use to point at a receipt and ask "what's the total here," so there's no separate app or button to find — it's additive to a tool you were likely already using.

## How does virtual try-on work in ChatGPT?

A Try On button now appears next to supported clothing and accessory listings in ChatGPT's shopping results, as [TechRepublic's rollout coverage describes](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-chatgpt-virtual-try-on-shopping-favorites/). Tap it, then take or upload a selfie, and ChatGPT generates an image showing roughly how that item would look on you, powered by OpenAI's current image model, ChatGPT Images 2.5, which the company says produces sharper detail and more precise edits than its predecessor. The one limit worth knowing before you rely on this for a purchase decision: the generated image shows style and color, not fit — it can't tell you whether a size will actually fit your body, a point [Tom's Guide also flags in its hands-on walkthrough](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt/you-can-now-virtually-try-on-clothes-with-chatgpt-before-you-buy-them-heres-how-it-works).

## What is Favorites, and how is it different from just bookmarking a link?

Favorites lets you save a product directly to your ChatGPT Library or sort it into folders, from inside the same shopping flow where you found it — available on both mobile and web. The difference from a browser bookmark is that a saved favorite stays inside the same conversational context: you can come back and ask ChatGPT to compare your saved items, check if a price dropped, or find something similar, instead of re-explaining what you were looking for from scratch.

## How does ChatGPT handle the camera photos it saves?

ChatGPT stores reference photos — the selfies used for try-on — so future try-on requests don't need a fresh photo each time. You can review, replace, or delete these under Settings, Personalization, Reference Photos. The setting worth flagging plainly: this storage is opt-out, not opt-in, meaning the photos are kept by default until you go find that menu and remove them yourself, rather than ChatGPT asking permission to keep them in the first place. That default matters more for a selfie than for most other data ChatGPT holds, since a reference photo is tied to your actual appearance in a way a text conversation isn't — treat it the same way you'd treat any biometric-adjacent data you hand to a cloud service, not as a throwaway convenience setting.

```text
Settings > Personalization > Reference Photos
  - Review saved selfies used for virtual try-on
  - Replace with a new photo
  - Delete to stop future try-on requests from reusing it
```

## How do you turn these features on?

Nothing to enable separately — the camera scanning and shopping updates are live inside the existing camera button in the ChatGPT app on iOS and Android, and Favorites appears automatically in shopping results once you're on a current app version. If you don't see Try On or Favorites yet, updating the app is the first thing to check, since OpenAI has a history of staggered rollouts across regions and account tiers for shopping-adjacent features. A second thing worth checking: Try On currently appears only next to listings ChatGPT itself has marked as shoppable, so a plain product photo you scan yourself won't trigger the button — it only shows up inside ChatGPT's own curated shopping results.

## Is this actually useful, or is it shopping-feature theater?

The document scanning half is a genuinely useful, low-stakes convenience — multi-page auto-combine saves real manual steps for anyone dealing with receipts or forms, and nobody's privacy calculus changes much over a scanned contract. Virtual try-on and Favorites are a different story: they're OpenAI building e-commerce muscle directly into ChatGPT's core interface, and the opt-out photo retention is the detail that deserves more scrutiny than the demo-friendly Try On button gets. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does ChatGPT's new camera scanning feature do?

It captures multiple pages in sequence and automatically combines them into a single document, with sharper edge detection and glare handling than before. It lives inside the camera button ChatGPT already had, rolled out around October 1, 2026.

### How accurate is ChatGPT's virtual try-on for clothes?

It shows style and color reasonably well using a selfie and OpenAI's ChatGPT Images 2.5 image model, but it cannot confirm fit or sizing — treat it as a style preview, not a fitting-room substitute.

### Does ChatGPT keep the photos I use for virtual try-on?

Yes, by default. ChatGPT saves reference photos for future try-on requests unless you remove them yourself under Settings, Personalization, Reference Photos — the retention is opt-out, not opt-in.

### Do I need to enable anything to get the new camera features?

No. The scanning and shopping updates are live inside the existing camera button on a current version of the ChatGPT app for iOS and Android; if they're missing, updating the app is the first fix to try.
