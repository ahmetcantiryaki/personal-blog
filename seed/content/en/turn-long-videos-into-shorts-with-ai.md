Short answer: to turn a long video into Shorts with AI, run it through a clipping tool that auto-detects highlight moments, adds captions and vertical reframing, then review each clip for its hook before publishing. AI compresses hours of footage into 10–15 candidate clips in minutes; deciding which one is actually worth posting is still a human call.

## How do you turn a long video into Shorts with AI?

The process has four steps: upload the recording, let the AI detect highlight moments, apply automatic captions and vertical reframing to the clips you pick, then review each one for its hook. Tools like Opus Clip combine all four steps in one interface — a one-hour podcast episode can produce 10–20 candidate clips in a single pass.

The real time savings happen in the first three steps. Manually scrubbing through an hour of video to mark the good parts takes hours by itself; AI does it in minutes. The fourth step — deciding which clip will actually land — stays human work, and it should.

## How does AI detect highlight moments?

Moment-detection engines, like Opus Clip's ClipAnything 2.0, analyze visual, audio, and sentiment cues together to find clip-worthy moments across any content type, from interviews to gaming footage. Each candidate clip gets a "Curation Score" from 0 to 100, based on content signals, speaker energy, and pacing, meant to predict viral potential.

A high score doesn't always mean the right clip — the model sometimes rates a high-energy but out-of-context moment highly. Treating the score as a first-pass filter, then making the final call by watching the clip yourself, produces more reliable results than trusting the score alone.

## How reliable is automatic captioning and vertical reframing?

Auto captions run at roughly 94% accuracy on clear English audio; accuracy for other languages varies with recording quality and tends to run a bit lower. The 2026 updates brought multi-language caption support across 20-plus languages and improved speaker detection; keyword highlighting, where individual words light up as they're spoken, is now standard on all paid plans.

That 94% accuracy drops noticeably on noisy recordings or sections where multiple people talk over each other. Watching each clip's captions at least once before publishing is the only reliable way to catch errors, particularly in sentences with names, brand terms, or numbers.

## Which source videos clip most efficiently?

Formats that naturally contain multiple distinct ideas — interview-style podcasts, panel discussions, live Q&A sessions — yield more usable clips than a single person's long-form monologue. The reason is simple: a dialogue naturally produces "striking line" moments (an objection, an example, a number), while those moments are rarer and more scattered in a monologue.

Keeping this in mind while planning a recording makes clipping easier later: asking a podcast guest, from time to time, "can you sum that up in one sentence" creates natural clip points the AI can flag.

## What is hook-first re-editing, and how do you do it?

In the original recording, an idea usually opens with a setup sentence; in short-form, that order often needs flipping. Taking the clip AI selected and moving its most striking line — usually spoken somewhere in the middle — to the front decides whether a viewer stays past the first two seconds. This is the one step most automated clipping tools still leave to a human.

## Do you need separate exports for Shorts, Reels, and TikTok?

All three platforms use a similar vertical format (9:16), but optimal length and caption placement differ by platform: TikTok tends to reward slightly longer clips (60–90 seconds), while Shorts and Reels perform better with tighter 15–30 second cuts. Modern clipping tools ship platform-specific export presets, so exporting one clip in three different lengths and caption positions for three platforms takes a few clicks. ## How do you build a repeatable weekly batch process?

Once a week, upload your long recording — a podcast episode, a livestream, a webinar — let AI find the highlight moments, download the 8–10 highest-scoring clips, then review all of their hooks and captions in one sitting. That's far less time than editing a video from scratch every single day, and it produces enough clips to fill a week's content calendar. As of 2026, many tools integrate directly with scheduling platforms like Buffer and Later, letting you schedule straight from the clipping tool instead of downloading and re-uploading.

## Where does AI clipping still need a human eye?

Three places: hook selection (moving the most striking line to the front), context checking (cutting a moment the AI scored highly but that's actually off-topic), and brand safety (keeping a joke that could be misread, or a sensitive topic, from becoming the clip that gets pushed out). Skipping these three checks and publishing AI output as-is saves time short-term but erodes brand voice over time.

A practical rule of thumb: if you're spending more than 3–5 minutes of human time per clip, you're probably either editing the wrong clip or manually redoing work AI already handled well, like captions or reframing. Either way, it's more efficient to step back and decide whether to accept the AI's output as-is or discard that clip entirely and move to the next one on the list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How accurate are AI-generated captions for video clipping?

Auto captions run at roughly 94% accuracy on clear English audio. Accuracy drops on noisy recordings or overlapping speech, so watching each clip's captions before publishing is necessary to catch errors.

### How do AI clipping tools decide which moments are highlights?

A moment-detection engine analyzes visual, audio, and sentiment cues together and assigns each candidate clip a Curation Score from 0 to 100 based on content signals and speaker energy. A high score doesn't always mean the right clip, so a final human review still matters.

### How many Shorts can you get from one podcast recording?

A one-hour recording typically yields 8–20 candidate clips. Picking the 8–10 highest-scoring ones and reviewing their hooks and captions is usually enough to fill a week's content calendar.

### Should you use the same clip for TikTok, Shorts, and Reels?

The vertical 9:16 format can stay the same, but optimal length differs: TikTok tends to reward 60–90 second clips, while Shorts and Reels perform better with tighter 15–30 second cuts. Modern tools offer platform-specific export presets to handle this automatically.
