Short answer: Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on the evening of November 18, 2026 — above its $70–78 range — raising about $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record. Trading in ANT starts today, November 19, on the Nasdaq. Nothing changes for Claude's API pricing or access because of it.

That's the whole story for anyone who just wants the number. The more useful question, if you're building on Claude or paying for it, is what a public listing of this size actually changes about the company behind the model — and what it doesn't.

## What Price Did Anthropic's IPO Actually Price At?

Anthropic's IPO priced at $83 per share, above the $70–78 indicative range set in the amended S-1 it filed on November 10, 2026. The deal sold 980 million Class A shares in total: 956.35 million sold directly by Anthropic and 23.65 million sold by existing stockholders, mainly the company's co-founders cashing out a small slice of their stake.

At that price, the offering raised roughly $81.3 billion before underwriting fees. Anthropic is also raising a separate $26 billion through a private placement alongside the public offering, and underwriters hold a 30-day option to buy up to about 147 million additional shares if demand justifies it — the standard over-allotment clause known as a greenshoe.

Pricing above the range wasn't a surprise by the end of the process. Bloomberg reported in mid-November that order books were running at roughly six times the number of shares on offer, which is the kind of demand that typically pushes a deal above its stated range rather than to the low end of it.

## Did This Break the IPO Record?

Yes — reports put Anthropic's $81.3 billion raise above the previous record, held by SpaceX's June 2026 listing. Coverage is less precise about SpaceX's exact number: some reports cite roughly $75 billion for the base offering, others put the total closer to $85–86 billion once SpaceX's own over-allotment option is included, so treat the comparison as "largest base offering on record" rather than a clean, universally agreed-upon figure.

What's clear either way: an $81.3 billion raise, at a reported fully diluted valuation around $1.5 trillion, is one of the largest technology listings ever, full stop — the SpaceX comparison is a detail about which record it broke, not whether it's historic.

## Does This Change Claude's API Pricing or Access?

No, not directly. Going public changes who owns Anthropic's stock and how the company reports its finances; it does not, by itself, touch Claude's API rate limits, model pricing, or product roadmap. If you're running production workloads on Claude Sonnet, Opus, or Haiku today, your contracts, pricing tiers, and usage limits are unaffected by the IPO closing on November 20.

What the raise does change, less directly, is Anthropic's ability to keep funding the compute and research that produced releases like Claude Haiku 5.5 and the Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 models. $81.3 billion plus the $26 billion private placement is capital specifically earmarked for staying competitive against OpenAI — which, notably, has not gone public — in a market where training and inference compute are the single largest cost line. That's a real, if indirect, reason this matters to developers: a better-capitalized Anthropic is less likely to need to cut corners on model quality or infrastructure reliability to manage cash.

## Who Controls Anthropic Now That It's Public?

Anthropic will operate as a "controlled company" under Nasdaq listing rules, meaning its seven co-founders, together with the independent Long-Term Benefit Trust, retain voting control after the IPO closes. Public shareholders buying ANT stock get economic exposure to Anthropic's growth, but they don't get the voting leverage that would let them force the company to, say, prioritize quarterly margins over its stated safety commitments.

This is the detail worth understanding if you've been nervous that "going public" always means short-term profit pressure wins. It's a deliberate structural choice, not an accident: Anthropic set up the Trust specifically so public-market incentives can't override its public-benefit-corporation mandate. Whether that holds up in practice over years of market pressure is a fair thing to be skeptical about — governance structures constrain decisions on paper more cleanly than they do in a boardroom under pressure — but it's a meaningfully different setup than a typical IPO with no controlling shareholder at all.

## What's Confirmed, and What's Still Speculation?

Confirmed: the $83 price, the $81.3 billion raise, the Nasdaq listing under ANT starting November 19, the $26 billion private placement, and the controlled-company governance structure are all matters of public record from the pricing and the S-1 filings. None of that is in dispute.

Speculative: anything about how this capital specifically reshapes Claude's roadmap, pricing strategy, or competitive moves over the next year is a forecast, not a fact — Anthropic hasn't announced product changes tied to the IPO, and treating "they just raised $81 billion" as a prediction of what ships next quarter is reading tea leaves. The honest takeaway is that Anthropic now has substantially more capital to execute a strategy it had already set, not that the strategy itself just changed.

If there's one opinion worth stating plainly: the governance structure matters more here than the valuation headline. A $1.5 trillion number is attention-grabbing, but it's the Trust's continued control — not the size of the check — that will actually determine whether Claude's product decisions still look the same in two years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When did Anthropic's IPO price, and at what price per share?

Anthropic priced its IPO on the evening of November 18, 2026, at $83 per share — above the $70–78 range set in its amended S-1 filing from November 10. Trading in the stock, under ticker ANT on the Nasdaq Global Select Market, began the next day, November 19, with the offering set to close November 20.

### Did Anthropic's IPO break a record?

Reports describe Anthropic's roughly $81.3 billion raise as the largest IPO on record, surpassing SpaceX's June 2026 listing, though exact comparisons vary because sources differ on whether to count SpaceX's over-allotment proceeds. Either way, it's one of the largest technology IPOs ever by dollars raised.

### Does this IPO change Claude's API pricing or rate limits?

No. Going public changes Anthropic's ownership structure and financial reporting obligations, not its product pricing, rate limits, or API terms, which remain governed by Anthropic's existing pricing pages and enterprise contracts. Any future pricing change would be announced separately, the same way past changes like Claude Sonnet 5.5's cache price cuts were.

### Who controls Anthropic after the IPO?

Anthropic is a "controlled company" under Nasdaq rules, meaning its seven co-founders and the independent Long-Term Benefit Trust retain voting control even though the public now holds economic shares in ANT stock. This structure was built specifically to keep Anthropic's safety-focused mission from being overridden by short-term shareholder pressure.
