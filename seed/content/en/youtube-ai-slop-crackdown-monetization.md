Short answer: YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy strips monetization from mass-produced, templated AI videos, not from AI-assisted content with real creative input. Starting February 1, 2027, new channels need 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days to enter the Partner Program — double the 10 million bar existing members still use — and views now count from the first frame across every format.

## What actually changed in YouTube's AI content policy?

YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" guideline to "inauthentic content" and widened the definition from simple repetition to anything lacking genuine human creativity: templated videos with barely any variation, mass-produced uploads, and content that is trivially replicable at scale. In January 2026, that policy caught up with 16 major channels — a combined 4.7 billion views and roughly $10 million in yearly revenue — all removed from the YouTube Partner Program in one enforcement wave.

The line YouTube is drawing is not "AI or not AI." AI-assisted and even fully AI-generated videos remain eligible for monetization as long as they offer original value and aren't churned out from an identical template. What gets a channel demonetized is the absence of visible human creative input, not the tool used to produce it.

## What is the doubled Shorts monetization bar?

Beginning February 1, 2027, new channels applying for full Partner Program monetization will need 20 million qualified Shorts views over the trailing 90 days — twice the 10 million threshold that applies today. Existing Partner Program members are grandfathered in and keep the current 10 million bar; the higher number only hits channels applying fresh after that date.

"Qualified" views matter here more than the headline number suggests: YouTube excludes views it considers ineligible, so the public counter under a video can run well above the number that actually counts toward the threshold. Falling below the bar doesn't remove a channel from the Partner Program or touch long-form revenue — it only pauses the Shorts-specific revenue streams (the Creator Pool and Premium share) until the channel requalifies.

| Change | Who it affects | Effective date |
|---|---|---|
| Inauthentic content demonetization | All channels using templated/mass-produced AI video | Enforced since Jan 2026 |
| 20M qualified Shorts views / 90 days | New Partner Program applicants only | Feb 1, 2027 |
| 10M qualified Shorts views / 90 days | Existing Partner Program members | Unchanged |
| Views counted from first frame | All formats: long-form, live, Shorts | Since Aug 24, 2026 |

## Does counting views from the first frame change my earnings?

No — it changes what viewers see, not what you get paid. Since August 24, 2026, a view registers the moment playback starts, across long-form video, live streams, and Shorts alike, which inflates the public view counter creators can show in brand negotiations. Monetization and Partner Program eligibility still run on stricter engagement data, which YouTube Analytics now labels "Engaged Views" to keep the two numbers distinct. If your public view count jumped in late August with no change in revenue, this is why.

## What's the in-app shopping change for Shorts?

YouTube is rolling out frictionless in-app checkout on Shorts: viewers can tap a product a creator features and complete the purchase without leaving the app. Combined with the Shorts monetization overhaul, it signals where YouTube wants creator income to come from next — not just ad revenue share, but a cut of commerce that happens inside a Short itself.

## Does this affect creators outside the US?

The policy and thresholds apply globally to the Partner Program, but "qualified views" are calculated the same way everywhere — YouTube doesn't currently publish a region-by-region breakdown of what counts as ineligible. Creators in smaller-language markets have reported the 20 million bar feels steeper in practice, since a smaller addressable audience means each qualified view is harder to earn than in English-language content with a larger pool of potential viewers. If you're building a channel outside a major English-speaking market, budget more time to reach the threshold than the headline number suggests.

## How do you use AI on YouTube without getting flagged?

Three things matter more than which tool you use. First, toggle the "altered or synthetic content" disclosure in YouTube Studio whenever it applies — hiding AI use is a separate, faster path to a strike than using AI itself. Second, avoid identical templates across uploads: same intro, same voice cadence, same structure, video after video, is exactly the pattern the inauthentic-content policy targets. Third, keep a visible human decision somewhere in the pipeline — script edits, voice direction, a genuine narrative angle — because that's what separates "AI-assisted" from "AI slop" in enforcement terms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does using AI to make YouTube videos get you demonetized?
Not by itself. YouTube demonetizes content under its "inauthentic content" policy only when it's templated, mass-produced, or trivially replicable with no visible human creative input — AI-assisted videos with genuine editorial work remain eligible for the Partner Program.

### How many Shorts views do you need to make money on YouTube in 2027?
Existing Partner Program members need 10 million qualified Shorts views in the trailing 90 days, unchanged from today. New channels applying for the Partner Program starting February 1, 2027 need 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, double the current bar.

### Why did my YouTube view count jump without more revenue?
Because YouTube started counting views from the first frame of playback on August 24, 2026, across all formats, which raises the public counter. Monetization still runs on stricter engagement data, now labeled "Engaged Views" in Analytics, so earnings don't move with the public count.

### What happens if my channel falls below the Shorts monetization threshold?
Falling below the qualified-views threshold pauses only the Shorts-specific revenue streams — the Creator Pool and YouTube Premium share — until the channel requalifies. It doesn't remove the channel from the Partner Program or affect earnings from long-form video.
