Positioning is the strategic decision about who your product is for and what alternative it replaces; messaging is the copy — the headline, the taglines, the campaigns — that expresses that decision. Skip positioning and write your homepage copy first, and you end up polishing sentences that have nothing solid underneath them.

## Why Should You Position Before You Write Copy?

Positioning has to come first because messaging is an output of positioning, not a substitute for it — you cannot write copy that converts if you haven't decided who you're for and what you're up against. A common mistake founders make is treating their homepage headline as their positioning; the headline is one expression of positioning, aimed at one audience, in one context, but positioning itself is the underlying strategic call about where your product fits in the market.

Start every positioning exercise by asking: what would your best customers use instead of you if you didn't exist? Not your closest competitor — the actual alternative your best-fit customers would default to, which is sometimes a spreadsheet, sometimes a competitor, sometimes "doing nothing."

## What Is April Dunford's Five-Component Positioning Framework?

April Dunford's framework works through five components in a fixed order: competitive alternatives first, then your unique attributes relative to those alternatives, the value those attributes unlock, your target market's characteristics, and finally the market category you're claiming. Each component builds on the one before it — you can't identify your unique value until you know what you're being compared against, and you can't pick a category until you know which customers you're positioning for.

The critical discipline in this framework is sourcing every component from your best-fit customers' actual language and buying behavior, not from what your team assumes. Interview the customers who adopted fastest and expanded the most, and build positioning from what they say made you different — not from a features list.

## How Do You Find a Differentiated Value Claim?

A differentiated value claim sits at the intersection of what you do better than the alternative and what your target market actually cares about — a true differentiator nobody values is worthless, and a valued benefit everyone offers isn't a differentiator. Test each candidate claim against both filters before it goes anywhere near your homepage.

The fastest way to find real differentiators is asking recently won customers, in their own words, why they picked you over what they were using before — not what features they use, but what changed their mind at the moment of decision. ## What's the Right Homepage Message Hierarchy?

A converting homepage leads with the outcome the visitor wants, not the feature that produces it — the headline names the result, the subhead explains how you get there, and only the body copy or a lower section lists specific features as proof. Visitors decide whether to keep reading in seconds, so the hierarchy has to answer "is this for me and does it solve my problem" before it earns the right to explain how.

| Section | Job | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | State the outcome | Naming a feature instead of a result |
| Subhead | Explain the mechanism | Restating the headline with different words |
| Proof section | Back the claim with evidence | Generic logos with no context |
| Feature list | Show how the outcome happens | Leading with this instead of ending with it |
| CTA | One clear next step | Multiple competing CTAs |

## Should You Lead With Features or Outcomes?

Lead with outcomes in every customer-facing surface, and hold features in reserve as proof once the visitor already believes the outcome is real and relevant to them. Features answer "how," outcomes answer "why should I care" — and visitors decide whether they care before they decide whether they believe the how.

This doesn't mean hiding features; a technical buyer specifically wants to verify the how before trusting the why. It means sequencing: outcome first to earn attention, features second to earn belief.

## How Do You Handle Proof and Objections in Your Messaging?

Proof needs to be specific to the outcome you claimed — a case study that names the metric that moved, not a generic testimonial about how helpful your team was — and objections need to be addressed before the visitor has fully formed them, usually right next to the claim most likely to trigger the doubt. If your product is more expensive than the alternative, address the ROI math near the pricing section rather than hoping nobody asks.

## How Do You Name a Category?

Naming a category is itself a positioning decision, not a marketing flourish: claiming an existing, well-understood category borrows its buyer intent and budget line but forces you to compete on its terms, while creating a new category gives you the terms but costs you the education budget category-creation requires. Most early-stage SaaS companies are better served by positioning inside an existing category with a clear differentiator than by inventing a new one nobody is searching for yet — and getting pricing wrong for that category undercuts good positioning fast, which is why it's worth reading alongside our list of [common SaaS pricing mistakes](/en/posts/saas-pricing-founder-mistakes).

## How Should You Test Messaging With Real Prospects?

Test messaging the same way you'd test a product feature: put two or three candidate headlines in front of real prospects — through sales calls, user interviews, or a simple A/B test on paid traffic — and watch which one prospects repeat back in their own words when asked what your product does. Messaging that prospects can paraphrase accurately after one read is messaging that will convert; messaging that gets a blank stare needs another pass, no matter how much the team likes it internally.

## Can You Use AI to Draft Messaging Variations?

Yes, and it's one of the better uses of AI in marketing: feed a language model your positioning inputs (alternatives, differentiators, target customer language) and generate a dozen headline variants in minutes, then apply the same brutal filter you'd apply to human-written drafts — cut anything vague, anything that could describe a competitor equally well, and anything that leads with a feature instead of an outcome. AI is fast at generating options and useless at judging which one is true to your actual positioning; that judgment call stays yours.

## Positioning Worksheet

1. **Competitive alternatives** — what would your best customers use instead of you?
2. **Unique attributes** — what do you have that those alternatives don't?
3. **Value and proof** — what does that attribute enable, and how do you prove it?
4. **Target market** — which customers care most about that value?
5. **Market category** — what frame makes your value obvious to that market?

## Homepage Message Template

- Headline: [Outcome] for [target customer]
- Subhead: How you get there, in one sentence
- Proof: One specific, named result
- Features: Three items that explain the "how," in service of the outcome above
- CTA: One action, stated once

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between positioning and messaging?

Positioning is the strategic decision about who your product is for and what alternative it replaces in the market; messaging is the copy — headlines, taglines, campaign language — that communicates that positioning to a specific audience. Positioning is the input; messaging is the output, and you can't write good messaging without positioning underneath it.

### Should my homepage headline lead with a feature or an outcome?

Lead with the outcome the visitor wants, not the feature that produces it. Features answer "how it works," which only matters once the visitor already believes the outcome is real and relevant to them — so outcomes earn the read, and features earn the belief that follows.

### How do I find my product's real differentiators?

Ask customers who adopted fastest and expanded the most, in their own words, why they picked you over what they used before. A real differentiator sits at the intersection of what you do better than the alternative and what your target market actually values — not every true difference is valuable, and not every valued feature is unique to you.

### Can AI write my SaaS messaging for me?

AI can generate headline and copy variants quickly once you feed it your positioning inputs — competitive alternatives, differentiators, and target customer language — but it can't judge which variant is actually true to your positioning. Use it to widen the option set, then apply your own filter to cut anything vague or equally true of a competitor.
