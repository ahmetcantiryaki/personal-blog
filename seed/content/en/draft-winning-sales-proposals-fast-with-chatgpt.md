Short answer: pour your discovery call notes into a clean template (problem, outcome, scope, price, proof, next step), hand that template plus your notes to ChatGPT for a first draft, tailor the tone to the buyer, present pricing with options, and verify every number by hand before you send. That loop cuts drafting time from hours to minutes compared to starting from a blank page.

## Why does messy discovery note-taking actually delay the proposal?

Scattered notes from a sales call — half-sentences, numbers spread across different tabs, details jotted down as "I'll clean this up later" — are the single biggest time sink once you sit down to write the proposal. Pouring notes into one clean structure right after the call (ideally the same day) both captures detail while it's fresh and cleans up the input you'll hand to ChatGPT. A messy input never produces a clean draft; skipping this step slows down every step that follows.

In practice, that means a 10-minute "note cleanup" break right after the call: sorting raw notes into the template's six headings, flagging any number that's still missing (to be verified later), and keeping anything said on the call but not yet in a written agreement on its own line. That 10 minutes saves hours once you're actually drafting.

## What template can you actually reuse on every proposal?

A six-part template reuses across most B2B proposals: problem (the concrete issue the buyer has right now), outcome (the state they reach once it's solved), scope (exactly what gets delivered), price (with options), proof (a concrete result from a similar client), and next step (a single clear action — a signature, a meeting, an approval). This structure means you fill the same skeleton after every call — you don't have to re-explain the format to ChatGPT each time.

```text
TEMPLATE — Sales Proposal Skeleton
1. Problem: [the buyer's concrete issue right now]
2. Outcome: [the state they reach once solved, with a number if measurable]
3. Scope: [exactly what's delivered, and what isn't]
4. Price: [2-3 options, each tied to a clear deliverable]
5. Proof: [a concrete result from a similar client]
6. Next Step: [signature / meeting / approval — one clear action]
```

## How do you actually prompt ChatGPT to produce the draft?

Pasting the template and your raw discovery notes together into [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com) and saying "turn these notes into a sales proposal draft following the template above, for [the buyer's role and company]" gives you a far faster entry point than starting from a blank page. [OpenAI's own help content](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001526) recommends the same approach for the ChatGPT add-in inside Word: paste the source notes or text directly into the prompt before asking for a draft. That add-in has been enabled by default since October 1, 2026.

There's a similar shortcut for discovery documents kept in Google Drive: since August 2026, Drive files can stay open next to the chat, so you can reference the file directly instead of copy-pasting notes out of it. That's especially useful for sales teams that keep discovery notes in a shared doc, cutting the drafting step down to a single reference.

If you recorded the discovery call as audio, the audio-upload feature added in October 2026 (on paid plans) turns the recording directly into structured notes — still worth reviewing for transcript errors, but much faster than transcribing by hand. Similarly, you can scan a multi-page notebook into a single PDF with your phone's camera (iOS first, since October 2026) and upload it straight into ChatGPT.

## How do you actually tailor the tone to the buyer?

The same template needs a different voice for different buyers: a technical team lead responds to numbers and integration detail, a finance manager responds to ROI and a payment schedule, a founder responds to speed and risk reduction. Telling ChatGPT "rewrite this draft for [the buyer's role], focused on [the point to emphasize]" lets you reuse the same skeleton for three different buyers in three different tones — you're changing the voice, not the template.

## Why does presenting pricing with options work better than a single number?

A single price gives the buyer only a yes-or-no choice; two or three pricing options (say, basic, standard, and expanded scope) moves the buyer from "should I buy this" to "which one should I buy." That small framing shift measurably affects close rate, because the buyer is no longer rejecting your proposal — they're just picking the option that fits their own budget.

| Option | Scope | Typical Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | A single core deliverable | A budget-limited buyer who wants to start fast |
| Standard | Core + 1-2 add-on modules | The middle ground most buyers pick |
| Expanded | Full scope + priority support | A larger-budget, long-term buyer |

## What do you do if the buyer says no, or goes silent?

Before you throw out a rejected or unanswered proposal, try to understand the buyer's real objection: is it price, timing, or a sense that the scope doesn't match the outcome they expected? Handing that objection to ChatGPT and saying "write a shorter reframe of the same proposal that addresses this objection" produces a faster second attempt than writing a new proposal from scratch. What matters is that the second draft goes through the same verification step — checking the numbers and references by hand again.

Silence is a different signal than rejection: the buyer might be busy, waiting on budget approval, or they simply forgot. A single short follow-up 3-5 business days after sending — not a new proposal, just a polite nudge on the same one — is usually enough. Telling ChatGPT "write a short, low-pressure follow-up email for this proposal" turns this step into a template too.

## What should you never skip before you send it?

The pre-send checklist is short but non-negotiable: verify every number (price, date, scope boundary) by hand, check the client's name and company details, and never let a concrete fact ChatGPT invented (a reference client's name, a statistic, a promised delivery date) go out unverified. This is exactly what you must never let AI invent: numbers and client-specific facts. The model can write fluently, but it doesn't know which number is correct — you do.

Privacy matters too once you're pasting client data into ChatGPT: if your discovery notes contain a client's internal data, pricing strategy, or personal information, check your company's policy before pasting it into a general-purpose AI tool. That applies just as much to [uploading audio to turn into notes](/en/posts/chatgpt-audio-uploads-to-notes) — a recording usually carries more incidental detail than typed text does.
