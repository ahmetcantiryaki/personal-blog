Short answer: OpenAI began retiring ChatGPT group chats on July 9, 2026 — you can no longer start a new one, your old history stays accessible, but a DM-style Messages tab replaced the feature. Gemini, meanwhile, never had a real group chat feature in its consumer product at all: a Google spokesperson has said Gemini "isn't meant for group chats" and, if added to one, will typically just leave it. So the scenario of "several people and an AI working together in the same thread" is a narrower space at the end of 2026 than it looked a year earlier.

That wasn't the expected outcome. ChatGPT group chats launched as a pilot on November 13, 2025 in Japan, New Zealand, South Korea, and Taiwan, and rolled out globally within a week. Eight months later, the feature was fully discontinued — a rare case of an AI company shutting down its own experiment, the opposite of the usual "broadcast channels reach everyone" playbook.

## What's happening to ChatGPT group chats right now?

As of July 9, 2026, you can't start a new group chat or join an existing one, but your old group chat history stays accessible. OpenAI replaced it with a DM-style Messages tab, shifting from an open multi-person thread to a more controlled, one-to-one communication model. Some guides dated earlier than OpenAI's own help page, like one from February 2026, still describe the feature as active — so always confirm current status against [OpenAI's official help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12703475-group-chats-in-chatgpt).

## Does Gemini have a group chat feature?

Not in any practical consumer sense. During Google Messages testing, a Google spokesperson said Gemini "isn't meant for group chats" and will typically leave a group if added to one. Gemini's real group-collaboration features live in Google Chat — but that's a Workspace product requiring a Gemini Business, Enterprise, Education, or Education Premium subscription. Even there, Gemini doesn't chat as a participant; it summarizes conversations and answers questions about them.

## Why did group chat fail so quickly?

Having several people talk to an AI in the same thread quickly creates context pollution: each participant's own intent, question, and follow-up mix into the same stream, and the model struggles to tell who it's answering and which instruction still applies. That doesn't mean the feature was technically broken — but many users who ran into a "noisy crowd" problem in daily use ended up going back to solo chats or a shared Project instead.

## What actually works for a team collaborating with AI today?

The model that works today isn't one open multi-person thread — it's a shared workspace: multiple people can access a Project in ChatGPT or a shared Cowork session in Claude, using the same context (files, instructions, history), but each person sends their own message at their own pace. That keeps the "everyone sees the same thing at once" benefit of a group chat while reducing the context-pollution problem. ## Who was actually using group chat before it closed?

ChatGPT group chats allowed up to 20 people in a thread, and the AI could post messages into the thread like any other participant. The most common use cases were low-stakes, fun tasks — a family planning a vacation, or a friend group asking the model to "settle the argument" over where to eat. Work usage was thinner; when a team tried to use a group chat to make a decision, the model not knowing whose instruction to prioritize caused problems quickly, which explains why the feature never stuck in professional use.

## What should you watch for when setting up a shared workspace?

When setting up a Project or a shared Cowork session, the first step is defining a clear "source of truth" file set: make sure everyone is looking at the same document and the same set of instructions, or each person ends up getting different answers from a model working off a different context. The second step is writing down upfront who has authority on what — "X has final say on budget questions" — because even a shared workspace can't resolve conflicting instructions on its own. The third step is cleaning the workspace regularly; once outdated decisions and stale instructions pile up, the model can get just as confused as a human participant about which information still applies.

## Three mistakes when kicking off a meeting with AI in the loop

The first mistake is adding the AI to a thread without a defined role — if the model is "everyone's assistant," it adds no specific value to anyone. The second is adding a new person to a thread with a long history — the model can misread old context and present outdated decisions to the newcomer as current fact. The third is leaving the decision moment to the thread itself — a group chat is good for gathering information, not for deciding; the final call needs its own clear, separate message.

My honest take: "group chat with AI" sounded natural on paper, but in practice it tried to paper over an unsolved problem — multi-person context — with a product feature. OpenAI walking it back within eight months is evidence this was a real usage problem, not a product preference.
