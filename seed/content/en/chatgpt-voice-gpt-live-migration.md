Short answer: OpenAI is retiring Advanced Voice and Standard Voice in ChatGPT on December 11, 2026, and every user moves to GPT-Live automatically that day. Your chat history, custom instructions, and voice choice all carry over without any manual action — but a few habitual workflows sound slightly different on the first try.

OpenAI's help center ties the change to its November 2026 release notes: GPT-Live launched in July 2026, voice usage has nearly doubled since, and most voice users are already on Live by default. Running two separate voice engines in parallel no longer makes sense once one of them handles almost all the traffic.

## When exactly do ChatGPT's old voice modes shut down?

Advanced Voice and Standard Voice are removed from ChatGPT Voice on December 11, 2026. After that date, starting a voice chat gives you GPT-Live and nothing else — there's no fallback to the old engines.

One exception remains: live video and screen-sharing continue running on the Advanced Voice engine until GPT-Live gains those capabilities. So while regular voice chat moves to Live entirely, camera-on video conversations keep the older engine a bit longer.

## What changes on GPT-Live, and what stays the same?

Your chat history stays exactly as it was; you can resume an old Advanced Voice conversation in text or voice. What changes is the engine itself: GPT-Live-1.1 and its mini variant, GPT-Live-1.1 Quip, have shipped since October 2026 with more natural accents and new voices across several languages.

Usage limits also differ by plan. As of November 2026, OpenAI's help pages list these figures, though pricing pages change often enough that checking the live page before relying on a number is worth it:

| Plan | Daily GPT-Live time | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Go | ~3 hours (mini model) | Capped to GPT-Live-1 mini |
| Plus | ~3 hours (full model) | Runs on GPT-Live-1 |
| Pro ($100–200/month) | 15 hours or unlimited | Varies by tier, verify |

Voice conversations inside custom GPTs stay on Advanced Voice until that GPT's own, separate deprecation date — so if you talk to a custom GPT by voice, test that workflow before December 11 too.

## Do my custom instructions and voice choice carry over automatically?

Yes — your account-level custom instructions and selected voice move to GPT-Live automatically; you don't need to re-enter them. Because GPT-Live runs a different engine, the same instructions can come out in a slightly different tone, especially ones that rely on specific emphasis or pacing.

The fastest way to check is to open GPT-Live manually today, before December 11, and run a two- or three-minute conversation with your existing custom instructions. If the tone feels off, you still have more than three weeks to rewrite the instruction for GPT-Live specifically.

## What's the pre-December-11 checklist?

Four steps cover the transition without surprises: open GPT-Live manually and try it once; test your custom instructions on the new engine; re-run the voice commands you use daily for notes and reminders; and check the separate deprecation date for any custom GPT you talk to by voice.

1. Open GPT-Live today and try a familiar task (e.g., "summarize my notes from tomorrow's meeting").
2. Re-test your custom instructions on the new engine; update them if the tone feels wrong.
3. List the three voice commands you use most and verify each one on GPT-Live.
4. If you use voice inside a custom GPT, note its separate deprecation timeline.

## How do you rebuild a daily voice workflow on GPT-Live?

Three workflows break most often: dictating notes, brainstorming on the move, and summarizing during a commute. All three work on GPT-Live, but adjusting your trigger phrases makes the transition smoother.

For notes, saying "list these three things as bullet points: X, Y, Z" works more reliably than a vague "take notes on this" — GPT-Live responds more consistently to explicit, structured commands than Advanced Voice did. For commute summaries, sharing an article or email and saying "summarize this in three sentences, then ask me questions" starts an active exchange instead of passive listening. For brainstorming, GPT-Live handles natural pauses better, so interrupting yourself mid-thought with "wait, add this too" causes fewer dropped threads than it used to.

## What's your fallback when voice misbehaves?

When voice misunderstands you or loses context, try resetting with a specific instruction first — "replay my last message and just do this" — rather than restarting the whole conversation; it's faster. The second fallback is switching the same conversation to text to make a correction there, since ChatGPT shares history seamlessly between voice and text.

A third option, for now, is leaning on the fact that Advanced Voice stays active specifically for video and screen-sharing tasks GPT-Live doesn't yet support. That overlap is temporary, but it's a working escape hatch today.

My own take: OpenAI's "no action needed" framing is technically accurate, but for anyone who's built voice into a real daily routine, burning the three-week test window is the safer move. The automatic switch works cleanly enough, yet the tone shift is noticeable if you never check for it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When do Advanced Voice and Standard Voice shut down completely?

Per OpenAI's help center announcement, both are removed from ChatGPT Voice on December 11, 2026, after which voice chat routes automatically to GPT-Live. Live video and screen-sharing remain an exception, staying on Advanced Voice until GPT-Live supports those features.

### Do I need to rewrite my custom instructions for GPT-Live?

No — your account-level custom instructions and voice selection carry over automatically without manual re-entry. Since the new engine's tone can differ slightly, testing your instructions once on GPT-Live before December 11 and adjusting if needed is still worth the few minutes it takes.

### Do voice conversations in custom GPTs retire on the same date?

No — voice inside custom GPTs follows that GPT's own separate deprecation schedule, and some enterprise workspaces may have a later date. If you use a custom GPT by voice, check its specific deprecation date rather than assuming December 11 applies.

### Is the daily usage limit on GPT-Live different from the old voice modes?

Yes — as of November 2026, Plus gives roughly 3 hours of GPT-Live-1 per day, while Pro offers either 15 hours or unlimited use depending on the tier. These figures shift on OpenAI's pricing page periodically, so confirm them there before the migration if your workflow depends on heavy daily use.
