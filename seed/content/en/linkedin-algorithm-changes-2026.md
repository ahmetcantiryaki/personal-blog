Short answer: LinkedIn's 2026 feed now weighs substantive comments, Saves, and Sends far above likes, treats dwell time as a gate for wider distribution, and ranks posts more by topic relevance to each viewer than by who follows whom.

## What actually changed in LinkedIn's algorithm in 2026?

Three shifts show up consistently across creator reporting this year: engagement quality is weighted over engagement volume, two new signals (Saves and Sends) now factor into distribution, and the feed increasingly ranks by topic relevance to the individual viewer instead of by raw connection count or follower graph. None of this is LinkedIn publishing a public ranking-factor list — it's pattern-matching from creators and marketing tool vendors watching their own reach data change — so treat specific numbers as directional, not official.

## Which engagement signals carry the most weight now?

Comments that extend a discussion — not just volume of comments — are the signal cited most consistently as carrying outsized weight relative to reactions. One vendor analysis from Dataslayer, published in February 2026, puts comments at roughly 15 times the algorithmic weight of likes; [creator-focused trend coverage](https://www.socialpilot.co/de/blog/linkedin-algorithm) through 2026 repeats similar rankings. Treat that specific multiplier as one analytics vendor's read on the data rather than a confirmed LinkedIn ranking weight, since LinkedIn hasn't published the figure itself.

| Signal | Reported relative weight | Added when |
|---|---|---|
| Comments (substantive, reply-provoking) | Highest — cited as ~15x a like in one analysis | Long-standing, reweighted in 2025-2026 |
| Saves | High — tracked in post analytics | Late 2025 |
| Shares with commentary | High | Long-standing |
| Sends (direct message shares) | Moderate-high — tracked in post analytics | Late 2025 |
| Reposts (no commentary) | Moderate | Long-standing |
| Reactions (likes and other emoji) | Lowest | Long-standing |

## What are Saves and Sends, and why do they matter?

LinkedIn added Saves and Sends to post analytics in late 2025, which is itself a signal: a platform doesn't start surfacing a metric to creators unless it's using that metric internally. Both actions require more deliberate intent than a reaction click — someone saving a post is signaling "I want to find this again," and someone sending it in a direct message is vouching for it to a specific person — which is exactly the kind of intent-weighted signal platforms have moved toward since the engagement-bait backlash of the early 2020s.

## Does dwell time really decide reach?

Multiple creator guides describe a pattern where LinkedIn first shows a post to a small slice of your network, then uses how long people actually stay on that post — not just whether they tap a reaction — as the gate that decides whether it spreads to a wider audience, with a 60-90 minute initial test window cited in several of these reports, including a [recurring monthly tracker](https://www.viralbrain.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2026-what-changed?page=5) that creator-tools vendors have published through the year. LinkedIn hasn't confirmed this mechanism publicly, so treat it as creator-observed behavior rather than documented policy, but it's consistent with how most major feed-ranking systems now test content on a sample before deciding whether to distribute it further.

For a creator, the practical consequence is that the first hour after posting matters more than it used to: replying quickly to early comments, and checking back on a post instead of publishing and walking away, can extend that initial test window's dwell time and comment count before the algorithm decides how far to push the post next.

## Does who you're connected to still matter?

Less than it used to, by every account tracking this. The feed is increasingly described as ranking by topic relevance to the individual viewer — inferred from what they've engaged with, their stated interests, and their professional context — rather than strictly by the connection or follower graph. Your network size still matters as one input, but it's reported as one signal among several rather than the primary gate it once was, which is why a post from someone with a smaller but highly relevant network can now outperform a much larger, less-targeted one.

## What should creators and company pages actually do differently?

Write the opening line to earn a comment, not a like — ask a real question or take a position someone wants to respond to, rather than ending on a generic "thoughts?" Format for the content to hold attention on the page itself (a carousel, a structured breakdown, a post worth re-reading) rather than one optimized purely for a fast scroll-past reaction. And don't chase reposts without commentary as a vanity metric — a share with added commentary is reported as carrying meaningfully more weight than a bare repost, so a repost-begging call to action is weaker than it looks.

## My take

The common thread across every reported change is that LinkedIn is pricing in intent, not just activity — a save, a considered comment, and a few extra minutes of dwell time all cost the user more effort than a reflexive like, and the platform is rewarding that effort because it's a better proxy for whether content is actually useful. Chasing like counts in 2026 optimizes for a signal the algorithm has already discounted.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What matters most for LinkedIn reach in 2026?
Substantive comments that extend a discussion are the signal most consistently reported as carrying the most algorithmic weight, followed by Saves and shares with added commentary — all weighted well above simple reactions like a thumbs-up.

### What are LinkedIn Saves and Sends?
Saves and Sends are post-analytics metrics LinkedIn added in late 2025, tracking when someone bookmarks a post to find later (a Save) or shares it directly with another person via message (a Send) — both signal stronger intent than a reaction click.

### Does LinkedIn still rank posts mainly by your connections?
Less than before. Creator reporting through 2026 describes the feed increasingly ranking by topic relevance to each individual viewer rather than strictly by the connection or follower graph, though network size remains one input among several.

### Does dwell time affect how far a LinkedIn post spreads?
Multiple creator guides describe LinkedIn testing a post with a small slice of your network first and using how long people stay on it, often cited as a 60-90 minute window, as a gate before deciding whether to distribute it further — a pattern LinkedIn hasn't confirmed publicly but one widely reported by creators tracking their own reach.
