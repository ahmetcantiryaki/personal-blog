Short answer: nail down the campaign's goal, audience, and budget yourself, then hand that framework to ChatGPT inside a Project and have it draft a dated content calendar across email, social, and ads, plus variant headlines and a UTM plan. Pricing, legal copy, and any promise you make to customers stay with you — the model speeds up the draft, it doesn't make the call.

## How Do You Scope a Holiday Campaign?

Pick one measurable goal first — revenue, new subscribers, or clearing inventory — because chasing three goals in the same campaign spreads the budget and the copy too thin. Separate your audience from last year's holiday buyers: cart abandoners need a different message than loyal repeat customers. Setting the budget against the goal rather than a channel percentage (say, "no more than $4 per new subscriber") also gives ChatGPT a concrete constraint to draft against instead of a vague brief.

## How Do You Build a Dated Content Calendar in ChatGPT Projects?

Open a Project and write the campaign's goal, date range, and channels as the project's custom instructions; those instructions apply to every chat inside that project, layered on top of your account-wide custom instructions. Asking it to "draft a daily content calendar for December 15 to January 2 across email, Instagram, and Google Ads, with date, channel, message theme, and CTA per row" produces a far faster first draft than starting from a blank calendar. Files shared in a project — your brand guide, last year's results — stay available across every chat in it, so you're not re-uploading the same documents each time.

## How Do You Generate On-Brand Variant Copy?

Add a short brand-voice file to the project — three example subject lines, a banned-words list, a tone description ("warm but not over the top") — and ask ChatGPT to stay inside that file on every request; without it, the model defaults to a generic, unbranded tone. Asking for five variants of a subject line or product description and A/B testing the top two is safer than asking for one "final" line — the model doesn't know which one will convert, you find that out from the data.

| Asset | Ask ChatGPT for | You verify |
|---|---|---|
| Content calendar | Date + channel + theme + CTA rows | Dates don't collide with actual inventory/logistics |
| Subject line / ad copy | 5 variants, on-brand per the voice file | Price, discount percentage, end date are correct |
| Budget draft | Percentage split by channel, scenarios | Compares against real historical CPA/ROAS |
| UTM plan | Source/medium/campaign naming template | Matches your existing naming standard |

## How Do You Draft a Budget and Channel Split?

Giving ChatGPT last year's actual spend and results per channel and asking it to "suggest three budget-split scenarios based on this data" is more reliable than asking for an estimate from scratch, because the model starts from a real baseline instead of a guess. Don't approve any scenario without comparing it to your own historical ROAS — the model doesn't know real cost shifts specific to your platforms, like CPMs climbing during the holidays, so you have to add that in yourself.

## How Do You Set Up a UTM Plan and Post-Campaign Review?

Before the campaign launches, have ChatGPT draft a fixed UTM naming template (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign=holiday2026`) and share it with every team member and agency involved — as [the UTM naming mistakes wrecking your reports](/en/posts/the-utm-naming-mistakes-wrecking-your-reports) lays out, inconsistent naming makes the data unusable once the campaign ends. Draft the post-campaign review template now too: goal vs. actual, cost per channel, top three performing variants — so writing the report on January 3rd means filling in a template instead of spending two hours staring at a Google Doc.

## How Do You Present the Campaign Brief to Stakeholders?

Once the calendar and budget draft are ready, turning them into a short deck instead of a long document to read in a meeting speeds up approval — the method in [building decks fast with ChatGPT Slides](/en/posts/build-decks-fast-chatgpt-slides) applies directly here: feed the same calendar and budget data from your Project to ChatGPT and ask for a 6-8 slide briefing draft (goal, timeline, channel split, budget, risks). Putting the three numbers stakeholders actually need to approve — total budget, goal, end date — on the very first slide turns the meeting into an approval, not a discussion.

## What Should You Never Hand to AI?

Write — or at minimum hand-verify at the final step — every statement that carries a fact: price, discount percentage, stock status, legal or warranty copy. ChatGPT will repeat a "40% off" figure consistently throughout a draft, but it isn't synced with your actual pricing system, so a wrong number can slip straight into a live campaign. The same rule applies when you [run a competitor teardown with ChatGPT agents](/en/posts/run-a-competitor-teardown-with-chatgpt-agents): the model hands you a list of observations, you decide which one actually becomes a campaign move.

Here's my take: use ChatGPT in this campaign like a fast assistant, not a copywriter with final say — let it produce the draft, but you sign off on every number and every promise.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you use ChatGPT Projects to plan a holiday campaign?

Open a Project and write the campaign's goal, date range, and channels as its custom instructions, which then apply to every chat inside that project. You can also add files — a brand guide, last year's results — to the project and share them across chats without re-uploading.

### Is it safe to ask ChatGPT for a campaign budget recommendation?

Only when you give it your actual historical spend and results and ask for scenarios based on that data; asking for an estimate from scratch misses cost realities the model doesn't know about, like CPMs rising during the holidays. Don't approve any scenario without comparing it to your own historical ROAS.

### What should never be left to ChatGPT in a holiday campaign?

Anything that carries a fact — price, discount percentage, stock status, legal or warranty copy. The model repeats these figures consistently in a draft, but they're not synced with your real pricing system, so a wrong number can easily go live.

### Should the UTM plan be set up before the campaign starts?

Yes. An inconsistent naming template makes the data unusable once the campaign ends; having ChatGPT draft a fixed `utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` template and sharing it with the whole team and any agencies before launch prevents that.
