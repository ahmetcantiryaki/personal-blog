Short answer: 60–90 seconds, not 15. As of September 2026, videos in that range outperform 15-second clips in average views and engagement rate on both TikTok and Instagram Reels. The old "keep it under 15 seconds" rule is dead; what actually drives performance is completion rate, not raw length.

## Why Doesn't 15 Seconds Cut It Anymore?

15-second videos now produce lower average views and engagement than 60–90 second videos on both TikTok and Instagram Reels. According to [Socialinsider's TikTok video length analysis](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/how-long-are-tiktok-videos/), this is the clearest trend shift of the past few years: users spend longer sessions in-app, and the algorithms now prioritize longer content that holds value over short filler clips.

The mechanism is simple. A 15-second video has to earn attention with an unusually strong hook or lean on a loop to hold watch time. A single 90-second video that keeps someone watching start to finish sends a much stronger signal to the platform: this person actually spent time with this content. Our piece on [how TikTok's 2026 algorithm decides reach](/en/posts/tiktok-2026-algorithm-reach) breaks down exactly how that signal shapes distribution.

## How Long Should a TikTok Video Be?

TikTok has two distinct sweet spots: roughly 24–38 seconds for viral content, and a separate 60–90 second range for deep-dive content. According to [The Content Labs' TikTok length guide](https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/how-long-should-tiktok-video-be), these two ranges serve different purposes and are not interchangeable.

The 24–38 second viral sweet spot works for fast, single-idea content opening with a strong hook. The 60–90 second deep-dive range is for educational and tutorial content: when the hook is strong, this length does not carry a major completion-rate penalty. In practice, a step-by-step tutorial or a software tip can safely run up to 90 seconds as long as the hook earns attention in the first three seconds.

TikTok's algorithm also rewards watch time held over raw view count. Per [Sociality's TikTok video length data](https://sociality.io/blog/tiktok-video-length/), a 90-second video that holds attention generates more algorithmic signal than a 15-second video that loops five times to fill the same total watch time. The system measures real attention, not a repeated loop.

## Does a Longer Video Work on Instagram Reels?

Yes, but Reels users scroll faster than TikTok users, so the sweet spot splits into layers. For quick-laugh and vibe content, the viral sweet spot is 7–15 seconds; for value content (tips, tutorials, lists), the sweet spot is 30–45 seconds.

The more striking data point: engagement rate on Reels climbs steadily as videos get longer, peaking at 60–120 seconds with a 4.7–4.8% engagement rate. That is more than double the 2.1% engagement rate of 15-second Reels. The assumption that "Reels must be short" doesn't hold up against the engagement data. Our guide on [fixing Reels hooks and retention](/en/posts/fix-reels-with-gemini-hooks-retention) covers how to strengthen the hook for these longer formats.

## Why Does Longer-but-Valuable Beat Ultra-Short?

Because completion rate, the percentage of viewers who watch to the end after starting, is TikTok's primary engagement signal. A 30-second video with an 80% completion rate outperforms a 90-second video with only a 30% completion rate.

That rule clarifies the real relationship between length and quality: length by itself is not an advantage, it only becomes one when the hook earns it. Shooting a 90-second video without a hook that grabs viewers in the first three seconds just burns extra runtime. Our piece on [how short video series drive retention](/en/posts/short-video-series-retention) applies this same logic to serialized formats.

My own take here is blunt: the "shorter always wins" myth is no longer supported by 2026 data. Length is a tool, not a constraint, and picking the wrong length for your content type means giving up the strongest signal you have on both TikTok and Reels — completion rate — before you even post.

## How Does Length Interact with Hook Strength?

Short answer: the stronger the hook, the longer a video can run. A video that establishes a clear curiosity gap or value promise in the first three seconds can hold its completion rate up to 60–90 seconds; a weak hook loses viewers even inside 20 seconds.

A simplified fit model looks like this:

```text
hook_score (1-10) x content_value (1-10) = safe_length_seconds (approx)

hook_score low (1-4):    safe_length = 15-24s
hook_score medium (5-7): safe_length = 24-45s
hook_score high (8-10):  safe_length = 60-90s
```

This isn't a scientific formula, it's a practical decision frame: when the hook is weak, keep the video short to limit the drop-off; when the hook is strong, extend the length to deliver more value.

## Platform and Content-Type Length Comparison

| Platform | Viral sweet spot | Value / deep-dive sweet spot | Long-format ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 24–38s | 60–90s | ~20 minutes |
| Instagram Reels | 7–15s | 30–45s (value) / 60–120s (peak engagement) | ~20 minutes |
| YouTube Shorts | 15–30s | 45–60s | ~3 minutes |

The table makes one thing clear: all three platforms now support a long-format option, but YouTube Shorts caps out far lower. TikTok and Reels have normalized 60–90 second runtimes, while Shorts' 3-minute limit keeps the format naturally constrained.

## Decision Guide by Content Type

For tutorial and educational content, target 60–90 seconds. Viewers already clicked to solve a problem, so a step-by-step walkthrough with a strong hook holds its completion rate at this length.

For quick-laugh and vibe content, target 7–15 seconds on Reels or 24–38 seconds on TikTok's viral format. This content carries a single moment or a single joke, and stretching it just drops the completion rate.

For list, comparison, and "5 tips" style value content, split the difference at 30–45 seconds. Each point delivers a new piece of information every 5–8 seconds, which keeps viewers watching.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a TikTok video be?

Target 24–38 seconds for viral content and 60–90 seconds for educational or deep-dive content. If the hook grabs attention in the first three seconds, extending to 90 seconds does not significantly hurt completion rate.

### Do longer videos work better on Instagram Reels?

Yes. Engagement rate on Reels rises as videos get longer, peaking at 60–120 seconds with a 4.7–4.8% engagement rate, more than double the 2.1% rate of 15-second Reels. Outside of quick-laugh and vibe content, longer formats generally outperform.

### Does short-form video still work in 2026?

Yes, but not for every content type. The 7–15 second range is still the viral sweet spot for quick-laugh and vibe content, but for educational and value content, 60–90 seconds now produces higher average views and engagement.

### What is completion rate and why does it matter so much?

Completion rate measures the percentage of viewers who watch a video to the end after starting it. It's TikTok's primary engagement signal: a 90-second video with a 30% completion rate underperforms a 30-second video with an 80% completion rate, regardless of raw view count.
