Short answer: LinkedIn Creator Marketplace is a discovery layer inside Campaign Manager, launched June 10, 2026, that lets brands search for creators by topic, audience, and post performance. It's still in alpha, limited to the US and Canada, and LinkedIn doesn't handle payment — brands and creators negotiate terms directly.

## What is LinkedIn Creator Marketplace?

LinkedIn Creator Marketplace [shipped on June 10, 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/how-b2b-brands-can-drive-impact-with-creators-and-stronger-creat), as part of a new "Content and Assets" section inside Campaign Manager. Brands use it to filter creators by expertise, audience makeup, and historical content performance, then shortlist who to approach. The tool is matchmaking, not contracting: pricing, deliverables, and timelines are negotiated outside LinkedIn, directly between the brand and the creator. [As ppc.land reported](https://ppc.land/linkedin-launches-creator-marketplace-and-brandworks-for-b2b-brands/), LinkedIn also launched a separate product the same day, BrandWorks, a managed professional-services offering — don't confuse the two. Creator Marketplace is a self-serve search tool; BrandWorks is a service LinkedIn runs on a brand's behalf. Shipping both on the same day splits LinkedIn's bet on the creator economy into two tiers: a cheap, self-serve discovery layer for brands that already have a marketing team, and a paid managed service for brands that don't.

## Why is LinkedIn investing in creators right now?

Because its own video and short-form content push is growing, and LinkedIn wants brands to work with existing creators instead of producing that content themselves. The "Content and Assets" section inside Campaign Manager doesn't just house Creator Marketplace — it also lets brands boost their own organic posts as ads from the same panel, part of a broader effort to manage organic and paid creator content in one place. Early interest skews toward enterprise software, fintech, and B2B SaaS companies, which historically invest in thought-leadership and technical content rather than classic influencer marketing — a sign that LinkedIn's definition of "creator" looks quite different from Instagram's or TikTok's.

## Who can actually use it right now?

As of November 2026, access is not open to everyone. The Marketplace is in alpha, restricted to US and Canada accounts publishing English-language content, and some reporting describes entry as invite-only. If you create content outside those two countries or in another language, you likely have no access to the Marketplace today, which is exactly why the rest of this guide focuses on what works whether or not you're in the alpha. LinkedIn hasn't published a timeline for expanding eligibility, and alpha-stage B2B products typically take months to widen. Early-user reports suggest the main gate isn't an application form but LinkedIn's own internal selection criteria, so there's currently no public path to "apply" — which, for now, favors larger accounts LinkedIn already recognizes over smaller, newer creators.

## How do you get discovered even without Marketplace access?

The answer isn't inside the Marketplace — it's in analytics every LinkedIn member already has. Click "View analytics" on any post and you get profile views, follower growth, clicks on your Custom CTA link, and a split between in-network and out-of-network reach. A high out-of-network share is the clearest signal that LinkedIn's algorithm is pushing your content past your existing followers, which is the exact kind of performance data brands — and eventually the Marketplace — filter on. The practical move: post weekly around one consistent topic, track those four numbers every time, and drop formats that tank your out-of-network reach, such as plain text over a document carousel. After three or four weeks a pattern usually emerges: if one format (a document carousel, say, or a short video) consistently beats the others on out-of-network reach, double down on it; if the metric doesn't move, change the format before you change the topic, since LinkedIn's distribution leans heavily on format, not just subject matter.

## How should brands actually use Creator Marketplace?

If your brand account has Content and Assets access in Campaign Manager, you can filter creators by topic, audience demographics, and past post performance to build a shortlist. What the tool doesn't do is message, brief, or pay creators for you; that negotiation happens off-platform. For measuring B2B impact, skip follower counts and track what a creator's post actually drives: profile visits, demo requests, or form fills tied to that content. Teams that already tune [how often they post](/en/posts/how-often-should-you-post-2026) around intent signals will recognize this measurement logic immediately. In practice that can be as simple as adding a "sourced from which creator post" field to your CRM; without tying a Marketplace creator's content to form fills that land weeks after the campaign ends, you won't see its real impact at all.

## Creator Marketplace vs. BrandWorks vs. a classic influencer platform

| Tool | Stage / coverage | Built for | What it handles | Payment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn Creator Marketplace | Alpha, US/Canada only, English | Brands + creators | Discovery and matching | Negotiated directly |
| LinkedIn BrandWorks | Generally available (managed service) | Brands | End-to-end professional service | Billed through LinkedIn |
| Classic influencer platform (Aspire, Grin, etc.) | Generally available, cross-platform | Brands + creators | Campaigns, payment, reporting | Through the platform |

The real difference isn't price — it's data access. Classic platforms collect performance data through their own SDKs or creator self-reporting; LinkedIn already owns it.

## Is it worth chasing right now?

My take: if you publish English-language B2B content from the US or Canada, yes — request access, since the cost is zero and there's no downside. For creators outside that scope, the near-term payoff is limited; the investment that pays off either way is the posting-and-analytics loop described above. It's not a coincidence that [LinkedIn document carousels](/en/posts/linkedin-document-carousels-that-get-saved) tend to post the save rates brands care about — the performance signal the Marketplace filters on ultimately comes from that same kind of consistent, saved content. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is LinkedIn Creator Marketplace?

LinkedIn Creator Marketplace is a discovery tool added to Campaign Manager in June 2026 that lets brands find creators filtered by topic, audience, and performance. Deal terms and payment are negotiated directly between the brand and creator, outside LinkedIn.

### How do I get access to LinkedIn Creator Marketplace?

As of November 2026, the tool is in alpha and limited to US and Canada accounts publishing English-language content; some reports describe access as invite-only. There's no public application form for creators outside those regions yet.

### Is Creator Marketplace the same as BrandWorks?

No. Creator Marketplace is a self-serve search and matching tool, while BrandWorks is a separate, paid managed service LinkedIn runs on a brand's behalf. Both launched the same day but serve different needs.

### How can I get discovered if I don't have Marketplace access?

Use the "View analytics" option on your posts to track out-of-network reach and CTA clicks, the same kind of performance data the Marketplace filters on. Posting weekly around one consistent topic is the fastest way to raise that out-of-network share.
