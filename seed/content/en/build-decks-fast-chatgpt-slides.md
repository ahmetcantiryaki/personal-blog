Short answer: to build a deck fast in ChatGPT Slides, give the audience, narrative arc, and target slide count in a single prompt — ChatGPT drafts the layout, and you edit it for data accuracy and brand consistency afterward. OpenAI rolled the feature out on October 16, 2026, but not to everyone: it needs a Pro, Business, or Enterprise plan with Space access turned on.

## What is ChatGPT Slides?

ChatGPT Slides turns a text outline into an editable presentation that lives inside ChatGPT's own interface, on web and in the desktop app. It's a different product from the older ChatGPT for PowerPoint add-in: that one is a sidebar that sits inside PowerPoint itself, while Slides runs in ChatGPT's Space environment and exports out to PowerPoint, PDF, or Google Slides.

## Who can actually use it?

ChatGPT Slides currently shows up only on Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans, and only if Space access is enabled on your account. A paid plan alone isn't enough — in company workspaces, an admin has to turn that setting on. [OpenAI's own help center article](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001605-creating-and-collaborating-on-slides-in-chatgpt) hasn't announced a rollout date for Free or Plus users, so as of October 2026, those two tiers are locked out. If you're not sure which ChatGPT plan covers which feature in general, our [complete ChatGPT guide](/en/posts/chatgpt-complete-guide-2026) breaks down what each tier actually includes.

## How do you actually structure the prompt?

An effective ChatGPT Slides prompt spells out four things: the audience, the narrative arc (problem → solution → proof → ask), a target slide count, and a tone. A vague "make me a deck" request produces a generic draft that takes longer to fix than writing a structured prompt in the first place.

```text
Audience: Series A investors, non-technical
Narrative: Problem (3 slides) → Product (4 slides) →
           Traction data (3 slides) → The ask (2 slides)
Slide count: 12
Tone: Short, data-first, no hype
```

That level of detail decides how many slides ChatGPT drafts and how much text lands on each one. Without it, the model guesses the slide count on its own and usually over-fills each slide with text.

Rather than expecting a perfect result on the first pass, asking for slide-by-slide revisions gets you there faster: "shorten slide 3, add a comparison table to slide 7" produces a far more predictable result than a vague "make it better." That's the same principle that applies to the model's general chat behavior — a vague instruction gets you a vague fix.

## Which kinds of decks does it actually suit?

ChatGPT Slides performs best on data- and text-heavy decks — investor updates, sales reports, quarterly reviews — because the real challenge in those decks isn't visual design, it's presenting information in the right order and the right density. For decks that demand heavy visual design, like a brand launch or a conference keynote, the draft is usually treated as a starting point a designer then reworks; the model isn't producing the final product there, it's speeding up the first step toward it. The payoff compounds for recurring decks — weekly status updates, sales demos — since the same template and narrative structure gets reused every week; get the prompt right once, and later weeks just need updated numbers.

## How do you keep brand consistency?

ChatGPT Slides defaults to its own design language on the first draft — it doesn't automatically pick up your brand colors, font, or logo placement. The reliable fix is to put your brand's hex color codes and font name directly in the first prompt, then review the draft slide by slide. If you've already built a [brand voice guide your AI will follow](/en/posts/brand-voice-guide-for-ai), the same tone rules carry over to the deck's copy — visual consistency doesn't come for free, but copy tone consistency does, if you give the model the rules upfront.

## How does exporting actually work?

On the web, you get three export formats: PowerPoint (.pptx), PDF, and Google Slides. In the desktop app, if PowerPoint or Keynote is installed, you can open the presentation directly in that app. OpenAI's own help documentation recommends checking both content and formatting after every export — slides with complex tables or charts can shift layout on export. [TechRepublic's hands-on review of the feature](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-how-to-use-chatgpt-slides-powerpoint/) echoes the same warning: layout drift after export is the most common issue, especially on slides with data tables or charts. On Windows, if the required MXC component isn't installed, the desktop app skips the collaborative Slides experience and works directly on a .pptx file instead — a real limitation if your team wants to co-edit at the same time.

## ChatGPT Slides or the PowerPoint add-in — which one?

The two products serve different situations, and the right pick depends on where you're already working and what file you're starting from. Building a narrative from scratch favors Slides; editing an existing .pptx favors the add-in.

| | ChatGPT Slides | ChatGPT for PowerPoint (add-in) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it runs | ChatGPT Space (web/desktop) | Sidebar inside PowerPoint |
| Plan requirement | Pro, Business, Enterprise + Space | Broad support, Free through Enterprise |
| Launch date | October 16, 2026 | May 21, 2026 |
| Export | PowerPoint, PDF, Google Slides | Already inside PowerPoint |
| Best for | Fast first draft from scratch | Editing an existing .pptx file |

## What does ChatGPT Slides still leave for a human to do?

The model hands you a fast layout and a first text pass, but data accuracy, visual quality control, and final brand sign-off stay your job. Any slide with financial figures, dates, or customer data needs a line-by-line check before it ships — the model can produce a confident-sounding but wrong number without flagging its source. For small teams, this fits the same pattern as our [AI-powered content marketing workflow](/en/posts/ai-content-marketing-workflow): the model adds speed, a human does the final check.

Here's my take: this is a draft accelerator right now, not a designer replacement. Getting the first 70-80% of the layout done in minutes is real time saved, but the last 20% — brand polish and data verification — is still on you. And that 20% is a moot point for Free and Plus users today, since they don't have the feature at all.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which ChatGPT plans include Slides?

ChatGPT Slides is currently available on Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans, and only if Space access is enabled on your account. As of October 2026, OpenAI hasn't announced a rollout date for Free or Plus users, and turning Space access on in a company workspace is usually an admin decision, not an individual one.

### How do I export a ChatGPT Slides deck to PowerPoint?

On the web interface, you can export your presentation as PowerPoint (.pptx), PDF, or Google Slides. In the desktop app, if PowerPoint or Keynote is installed on your computer, you can open the deck directly in that application.

### Is ChatGPT Slides the same as the ChatGPT for PowerPoint add-in?

No. ChatGPT Slides launched October 16, 2026, and runs in ChatGPT's own Space environment. ChatGPT for PowerPoint has existed since May 21, 2026, as a sidebar add-in inside PowerPoint. They have different plan requirements and serve different use cases.

### Does ChatGPT Slides use my brand colors automatically?

No, it defaults to its own design language. You need to include your brand's hex color codes and font name in your first prompt, then review the generated draft slide by slide to fix anything off-brand — otherwise the model picks a generic palette and typeface that has nothing to do with your brand.
