Short answer: AI Overviews is an AI-generated summary box embedded inside Google's normal results page, sitting above or between the blue links — you stay on the same page. AI Mode is a separate tab and a fundamentally different experience: you ask a question, get a synthesized answer, and can ask follow-up questions in a chat-like thread. The difference isn't size; it's the interaction model itself.

## What is AI Overviews?

AI Overviews is the AI-generated summary box that appears at the top of or within Google's classic search results page. It pulls information from multiple sources into a short answer, but you never leave the regular results page — the ranked blue links are still there, right below the box.

The box usually includes a few source citations you can click, and on some queries it can be expanded or collapsed. The core point stays the same: AI Overviews is a layer added on top of the existing results page, not a new page or tab. A user can skip it entirely and scroll straight to the organic listings.

## What is AI Mode?

AI Mode is a separate tab and a standalone surface in Google Search built for fully conversational, multi-turn search. You type a question, Google synthesizes an answer by pulling from many sources, and then you can ask a follow-up question in the same thread, with the system remembering what you just asked.

That's a fundamentally different interaction model from the classic "ten blue links" page. As of November 2026, AI Mode runs as its own dedicated tab in Google Search, and in some markets it behaves close to a default way of searching rather than an optional extra. Sources are woven directly into the answer; the traditional link list becomes secondary, because the user's attention is on the synthesized reply, not a list to scan.

## How is AI Mode different from AI Overviews?

The core difference is placement and interaction, not scale: AI Overviews extends the existing results page with a summary box, while AI Mode is its own search surface with a chat interface. The table below lines up the two side by side.

| Feature | AI Overviews | AI Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Where it appears | Inside the classic results page, above or between links | Separate tab, standalone search surface |
| Interaction model | One-shot summary, static box | Multi-turn, conversational follow-up |
| Context retention | Limited; each query is treated mostly on its own | Remembers prior questions and answers in the thread |
| Classic link list | Still visible, below or beside the summary | Secondary; citations are woven into the answer |
| Measured traffic impact | Reduces click-through rate | Experimental studies show an even larger click-through drop |

That last row matters: a study designed to test an AI-first Google search experience found it substantially reduced click-through traffic to publishers compared with the classic results page, as [reported by Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-mode-cuts-clicks-satisfaction-study-490494).

## How much is AI Mode and AI Overviews hurting publisher traffic?

The impact is uneven, and smaller sites are taking the biggest hit: according to Chartbeat data reported by Axios, small publishers saw roughly a 60% drop in Google search traffic in 2026, medium publishers around 47%, and large publishers about 22%.

In the same dataset, overall Google search traffic to publishers was down about 34% year over year, and Google Discover traffic fell roughly 15%. A separate picture emerges from a study by the Association of Online Publishers (AOP) covering eight leading UK publishers: news publishers fared comparatively better, with an average quarterly decline of around 5%, versus about 18% for the consumer and B2B segment. [Press Gazette's reporting on that study](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/search-isnt-dead-its-fragmenting-how-to-manage-google-traffic-decline/) projects that Google search traffic to leading UK publishers could halve by Q3 2027 if the current trend holds.

No single feature explains all of this — AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Google's broader zero-click trend are all pushing in the same direction at once. But the direction itself is unambiguous: the search results page is increasingly the destination, not the doorway.

## Does Google admit AI Mode and AI Overviews are hurting publishers?

No, Google disputes that framing. In a submission to the UK's Competition and Markets Authority (CMA), Google said there is "no realistic prospect of harm to publishers" from using their content to power AI search features. That's the company's stated position, and it sits in direct tension with the traffic data above — treat it as Google's argument, not a settled fact.

There's also a practical wrinkle publishers can't route around: according to Digiday's reporting, a site cannot opt out of having its content used in Google's AI search features without opting out of Google Search entirely. There's no granular setting that lets a site stay in classic search results while excluding itself from AI Mode or AI Overviews — it's all or nothing.

## Which sites are hurt the least?

Impact depends heavily on traffic mix: sites whose visits come mostly from branded, direct-navigation searches — people typing the brand name itself — are affected far less than sites that depend on long-tail informational queries. When someone searches a specific brand name, AI Mode or AI Overviews has little room to intercept that intent.

Service and utility journalism, along with generic how-to content, tends to take the heaviest hit, because those are exactly the queries an AI system can summarize completely in one paragraph. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is AI Mode the same thing as AI Overviews?

No. AI Overviews is a summary box embedded inside the classic Google results page, and you stay on that same page. AI Mode is a separate tab running a multi-turn, conversational search experience that lets you ask follow-up questions. They share the same underlying Google systems, but the interface and user experience are entirely different.

### How do I turn off AI Mode or AI Overviews?

There's no permanent, official setting to fully disable AI Overviews on the classic results page. AI Mode lives in its own tab, so you can simply avoid it by staying on the "All" or "Web" results tab when you search. Outside of rephrasing a query, there's no guaranteed way to prevent the AI Overviews box from appearing on a given search.

### How much does AI Mode reduce traffic to websites?

There's no single universal figure, but the direction is clear: Chartbeat data reported by Axios shows small publishers saw roughly a 60% drop in Google search traffic in 2026, and overall search traffic to publishers fell about 34% year over year. A separate experimental study, reported by Search Engine Land, found an AI Mode-style search experience cut click-through traffic noticeably more than the classic results page.

### Can I keep my site out of AI Mode but still appear in regular Google search?

No, that option doesn't exist. According to Digiday's reporting, Google doesn't let publishers exclude their content from AI-powered search features while still appearing in standard search results. A site is either visible across all of Google Search, AI Mode and AI Overviews included, or it opts out of search results entirely.
