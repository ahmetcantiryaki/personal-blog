Short answer: TikTok's 2026 algorithm tests a new video on your existing followers first, then expands it to the For You page based on completion rate and saves. Likes are now the weakest signal; saves, shares, and on-screen text recognition carry much more weight. A Local Feed added in February 2026 is a new way to reach nearby users without a follow relationship.

## Why do some videos explode and mine don't?

Because TikTok now checks how a video performs with your existing audience before showing it more broadly — this is called follower-first distribution. A new post goes to a small sample of your followers first; if completion rate and saves are strong enough there, the algorithm gradually opens it to a wider audience.

This means a small account can still go viral, but it also means the "test small, then expand" logic makes the first few seconds of your video critical — lose that first sample audience, and the algorithm never pushes the video further.

## What signals does TikTok's 2026 algorithm prioritize?

Ranking looks at three weighted signal groups: engagement signals (likes, comments, shares, saves — roughly 40% weight), content signals (captions, hashtags, sounds, on-screen text — roughly 35% weight), and user signals (watch history, device settings, account preferences — roughly 25% weight). Among these, completion rate, rewatch rate, and early engagement velocity stand out as the strongest ranking signals.

TikTok significantly increased the algorithmic weight of saves in 2026, after internal research showed saves predict content quality far more reliably than passive likes. When a viewer saves a video, they're signaling "I'll come back to this" — a much stronger intent signal than a quick like.

| Signal group | Approx. weight | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement (likes, comments, shares, saves) | 40% | Save count, share count |
| Content (captions, hashtags, sounds, on-screen text) | 35% | Keyword density in the first 50 characters |
| User (watch history, device, preferences) | 25% | Previously watched similar content type |

## Why do on-screen text and keywords matter?

On-screen text recognition is among TikTok's top 8 ranking factors — the algorithm reads the written text in your video to understand what it's about. Caption keyword density also matters, especially in the first 50 characters, since semantic relevance between a search query and caption text accounts for roughly 45% of ranking weight in search results.

This means editing a video should give the same care to on-screen text as to the spoken narration. A line like "3-minute bread proofing mistake" displayed on screen tells the viewer the topic upfront and helps the algorithm classify the content correctly.

## How should follower-first distribution change your content strategy?

Since a new video goes to your existing followers first, an account that posts consistently and has an engaged, loyal follower base gets a stronger initial test sample on every new video. That means protecting the engagement quality of your existing followers becomes just as important as growing follower count — a large but passive follower base can produce a weaker initial test performance than a small but active one.

A practical consequence: using formats like Stories and live streams regularly keeps your follower base counted as algorithmically "active," since those formats continuously refresh the platform's record of your audience's engagement. A follower base that never engages risks producing a lower completion rate during a new video's initial test phase.

## What is TikTok's new Local Feed?

Local Feed, added in February 2026, is a dedicated tab on the home screen that surfaces content based on the viewer's location, the post's topic, and recency. Posts from local businesses, restaurants, event organizers, and local creators can reach nearby users without requiring a follow relationship.

Creators don't need to do anything special for this — a standard location tag and topic-clear content are enough to appear in the Local Feed. ## A TikTok-SEO checklist

Run through this before publishing a video:

- Does the first 50 characters of your caption match a real search query?
- Does the on-screen text explain the video's topic within the first three seconds?
- Did you add a location tag (for Local Feed eligibility)?
- Do your subtitles or spoken narration overlap with the caption's keyword?
- Is there a clear question or claim in the first second that stops the scroll?

```text
Weak caption: "Did this today 😂 #fyp #foryoupage"
Strong caption: "Why do air-fryer potatoes stay soggy? The fix"
```

## How do comments and shares affect the algorithm?

Comment count alone matters less than the reply loop it triggers — videos where a creator actively responds to comments encourage viewers to come back to the video, which lifts the rewatch rate. Ending a caption with a clear question ("Which one would you pick?") can measurably raise comment rates because it gives the viewer a concrete prompt.

Shares are the fastest-growing engagement signal on the platform; a video sent directly through a message carries a far stronger intent signal than a like that shows up passively in someone's feed. That's why closing a video with "send this to a friend" makes a measurable difference, even though it looks like a small detail.

## How do you measure saves instead of views?

Check the "Saves" tab in TikTok Studio analytics more often than your view count; a save rate (saves divided by views) above 1% is a strong signal the algorithm is likely to push the video to a wider audience. Shares are also the fastest-growing engagement signal on the platform, so adding a closing line that prompts "send this to a friend" can make a measurable difference.

Completion rate, rewatch rate, and early engagement velocity are the strongest ranking signals. In the overall weighting, engagement signals (likes, comments, shares, saves) carry the largest share at roughly 40%.

### Why are saves worth more than likes on TikTok?

Because a save shows the viewer intends to return to the content — TikTok's internal research found saves predict content quality far more reliably than passive likes. That's why TikTok significantly increased the algorithmic weight of saves in 2026.

### How does TikTok's Local Feed work?

Local Feed, added in February 2026, is a tab that surfaces content based on the viewer's location, the post's topic, and recency. Posts from local businesses and creators can reach nearby users without a follow relationship; a standard location tag is enough to be eligible.

### Can a new account still go viral on TikTok?
