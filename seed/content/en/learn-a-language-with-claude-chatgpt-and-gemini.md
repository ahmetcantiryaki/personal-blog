Short answer: use all three together — practice pronunciation and fluency in voice mode, ask for grammar correction and vocabulary drills in text chat, and run a roleplay scenario a few times a week to simulate real conversations. None of the three is a perfect grammar source, so verify any correction you doubt against a second source.

## How do you set up an AI tutor persona for language learning?

Setting up a tutor persona means writing one system instruction at the start of a conversation that defines the assistant's role, your level, and its rules. For example: "You're a patient Spanish teacher speaking at a B1 level; gently correct every mistake and have me repeat new words in a sentence" keeps the tone and difficulty consistent for the rest of the session.

Writing this instruction once and saving it beats re-explaining it every session. Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini all let you persist this persona through projects or custom-instruction settings, so you don't redefine the tutor every time you open a new chat.

```text
Role: a patient Spanish teacher at B1 level.
Rule 1: Correct every grammar mistake, explain why in one sentence.
Rule 2: Make me use each new word in three different sentences.
Rule 3: Once a week, summarize my past mistakes when I ask.
```

## How do you practice speaking with voice mode?

Voice mode lets you hold a real-time spoken conversation instead of typing, which works far better than text chat for pronunciation, pacing, and natural pauses. [Claude's voice mode](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11101966-use-voice-mode) on web and desktop can use connected tools like Gmail, Google Calendar, and Slack mid-conversation, but Claude Code and Claude Cowork only support dictation, not full voice mode.

On the ChatGPT side, OpenAI's [GPT Live FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001274-gpt-live-faq) states the feature doesn't support video or screen sharing — not a problem for conversation practice, but limiting for scenarios that need visual context, like practicing an order from a menu you want to show the model. On Gemini's side, Google's [guide to changing Gemini's voice](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15277943) covers the voice settings used in Gemini Live, where you can adjust speaking pace and accent.

A practical method: start with a 10-minute voice session every day, force yourself to speak only in the target language, and ask the assistant to rate your speaking pace at the end.

## How do you combine correction with spaced repetition?

Correction alone isn't enough — you need to see the same mistake again a few days later to actually retain it, a technique called spaced repetition. The practical method: ask for a short list of your mistakes at the end of each session, then open the next session with "quiz me on my past mistakes" to pull that list back up.

All three assistants differ in how reliably they recall past conversations; notes saved inside a project or custom instructions are a more dependable memory source than plain chat history. For a language learner, that's the reason to write your mistake list into your own notes app — or your [AI-built second brain](/en/posts/build-second-brain-with-ai) — instead of trusting the assistant's memory alone.

## How do roleplay scenarios prepare you for real conversations?

Roleplay means giving the assistant a specific character and situation — a waiter, a hiring manager, an airport agent — so you rehearse real-world dialogue in advance. It's the same logic as [using AI to practice for a job interview](/en/posts/practice-interviews-with-ai-mock-sessions) applied to language learning: it gives you a low-stakes space to make mistakes before the real scene happens.

Three scenarios work well: ordering at a café, asking to change a hotel room, and introducing yourself in a job interview. Cap each scenario at five minutes and ask the assistant to steer it somewhere unexpected — an order that comes out wrong, for instance — so it tests your real-time reaction instead of a memorized response.

## How do the three assistants compare for voice practice?

None wins on every metric: ChatGPT's voice generally sounds more natural and expressive, Claude can take action with connected tools mid-conversation, and Gemini offers the deepest integration with Google apps like Calendar and Gmail. As the [AI voice assistants comparison](/en/posts/ai-voice-assistants-compared-gpt-live-gemini-claude) covers, which one is "best" mostly depends on which ecosystem you already live in.

| Criterion | ChatGPT (GPT Live) | Claude (voice mode) | Gemini Live |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice naturalness | High, expressive | Medium-high | Medium |
| Tool use mid-conversation | Limited, no video/screen share | Connected tools like Gmail, Calendar, Slack | Deep Google ecosystem integration |
| Inside Claude Code/Cowork | Not applicable | Dictation only, no voice mode | Not applicable |
| Best fit | Natural flow and expression practice | Task-driven, tool-assisted practice | Daily practice tied to Google apps |

## How much should you trust an AI tutor?

Don't trust grammar correction blindly — all three can produce a wrong but confident-sounding correction, especially in less common languages or advanced grammar rules. Verifying a correction you doubt against a second source — a dictionary, a grammar reference site, or a native speaker — matters most when error tolerance is low, like exam prep.

My take: AI tutors don't replace a human teacher, but they offer far more accessible low-stakes practice for 20 minutes a day than a human teacher ever could — the real difference comes down to whether you turn that practice into a disciplined routine.

## How do you build a weekly practice plan?

A simple plan: three days a week of 10-minute voice sessions, two days of text-chat correction and vocabulary drills, and one day of a 15-minute roleplay scenario. That totals roughly 70–80 minutes a week — below what most language-learning apps recommend, but effective because the repetition stays consistent. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which AI assistant is best for learning a language?

There's no single winner: ChatGPT's voice sounds more natural, Claude can take action with connected tools mid-conversation, and Gemini has the deepest integration with Google apps. Picking the one that matches the ecosystem you already use beats opening a new account just for this.

### Can I trust AI for grammar corrections while learning a language?

Partially — all three assistants get most corrections right, but they can produce a wrong, confident-sounding answer, especially on advanced rules. Verifying a correction you doubt against a dictionary or a native speaker is the safer approach.

### Is voice mode or text chat better for language learning?

Voice mode is better for pronunciation, pacing, and fluency practice; text chat is better for grammar correction, building vocabulary lists, and working through complex rules step by step. Using both in the same weekly plan gives the best result.

### Do roleplay scenarios actually work for language practice?

Yes, because they let you rehearse a real conversation's unexpected turns in a low-stakes setting before the real scene happens. Asking the assistant to steer the scenario somewhere unplanned — a wrong order, a canceled reservation — tests your real-time reaction instead of a memorized script.
