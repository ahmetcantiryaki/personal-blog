Short answer: growing on Substack Notes means posting a short, self-contained take, insight, or behind-the-scenes note instead of a bare link. That kind of post earns restacks, restacks surface you to other writers' followers, and a share of those readers follow you on Notes first, then subscribe to your newsletter. Link dumps get buried in the feed instead.

## How is Notes different from X and Threads?

Notes distributes through a recommendation engine and is wired directly into your subscriber base, while a post on X or Threads mostly travels through your follower graph. Per [Substack's own help center](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/14671398684308-What-is-a-restack-on-Substack-Notes), a restack shares a Note or post into your subscribers' Home feed and starts a new conversation thread there — that's the mechanism the recommendation system leans on to surface content beyond your existing followers.

That difference matters in practice: a viral post on X still needs a separate step (a bio link, a dedicated CTA) to turn into newsletter traffic. On Notes, the flow is already tied to your publication — a reader can discover you on Notes and subscribe for free in the same session, because the "Subscribe" button sits right on your profile.

One analysis argues Substack's discovery system rewards writers who stay inside the ecosystem: when a reader moves from your Note to a second publication without leaving Substack, that behavior is valuable to the platform too — so "stay active on Notes" isn't just a growth tactic, it lines up with how the platform's own incentives work.

## What content actually travels on Substack Notes?

What travels is a short take, a concrete insight, a teaser, or a behind-the-scenes note — not a bare link. Several writers' self-reported numbers point the same way: one says 62% of their last 500 subscribers came through Notes, but only 4% of those subscribed after reading a newsletter post first, which suggests native content is the real driver, not the click-through.

These figures are self-reported by individual writers, not audited by Substack, but the direction is consistent: engagement on a bare link post runs lower than on a short, standalone sentence, because the feed tends to deprioritize "exit" behavior — a link that takes a reader off-platform.

| Format | Feed performance | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bare link drop | Low | The feed deprioritizes exits off-platform |
| Short take or insight (one idea) | High | Reads fine with zero context, easy to restack |
| Teaser + newsletter link in a comment | Medium-high | Native content leads, the link is secondary |
| Behind-the-scenes note | High | Builds a personal connection worth restacking |

## How does the flywheel from one Note to a paid subscriber work?

The flywheel runs in four steps: a Note earns a restack, the restack surfaces it to the restacker's followers, some of those followers visit your profile and subscribe for free, and over time a subset of those upgrade to paid once they've seen enough value. One analysis argues the strongest conversion signal is ecosystem-native behavior: a reader who subscribes through a recommendation, engages with Notes, and then discovers a second publication converts at the highest rate.

The flywheel usually breaks when writers push the paid pitch too early. A paywall reminder aimed at a reader who hasn't even subscribed for free yet spends the trust the flow just built, before that trust exists.

## What's a realistic posting cadence for a solo writer?

Four to seven Notes a week, each built around one idea in a 10–15 minute writing session, is a sustainable solo cadence. One source attributes to Substack the claim that writers who post three or more Notes during their launch week see 50% more total subscriber growth — that figure is secondhand and hasn't been independently audited, so treat it as directional.

Writers who describe a daily Notes routine report gaining 500+ subscribers a month, but those numbers are also self-reported and often come from promotional posts; read them as "the direction is right, the magnitude is uncertain" rather than a guarantee.

## Does engaging with other writers actually earn restacks?

Yes, though most of the evidence is anecdotal rather than documented. One writer describes restacks as one of the algorithm's most important signals; another says restacking signals audience overlap to the recommendation system. Substack's own documentation doesn't publish these weights explicitly, so specific percentages aren't something to lean on.

The practical takeaway holds anyway: leaving real, regular comments on other writers' Notes in your niche, and restacking their work with your own quoted take, builds mutual visibility — which lines up with why [building community](/en/posts/build-community-not-followers-2026) outperforms stacking up followers over time. Per [Substack's publisher-side help article](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/30285743283860-What-happens-when-someone-restacks-my-post), a restack of your post can surface in the restacker's subscribers' Home feed and in a dedicated "Restacks" tab under your post's comments — a second, durable distribution channel beyond the original publish.

## How do you convert readers to your newsletter without being spammy?

Conversion works best as a natural invitation that follows a valuable Note, not a link bolted onto every post — a comment like "I wrote the long version of this" or the always-visible "Subscribe" button on your profile is usually enough. A bare "subscribe to my newsletter" call, posted as a standalone Note, tends to underperform because the feed reads it as an exit link.

Once your newsletter has grown, moving readers to channels you actually own — like [broadcast channels and DMs](/en/posts/broadcast-channels-dms-own-audience) — protects you against future algorithm changes. Let Notes do the discovery; let your email list carry the long-term relationship.

## What are the three most common mistakes on Notes?

First, auto-cross-posting from X: copying an X post into Notes unchanged reads as off-platform voice and underperforms, which runs against the "native format per platform" principle behind a good [cross-platform posting system](/en/posts/cross-post-multi-platform-system). Second, posting without a native hook — a Note whose first line makes no sense without outside context doesn't stop the scroll. Third, pitching the paid tier too early: asking for a paid upgrade before free value has been proven pushes away a reader who hasn't built trust yet.

## How much time should you actually spend on Notes?

My honest take: Notes works best as the free discovery layer of a broader [newsletter growth and monetization](/en/posts/grow-monetize-newsletter-2026) strategy, not as your only growth channel — Substack's own figures put Notes volume at roughly 12x its 2023 launch level, which means competition for attention has grown by a similar order. A few hours a week of native Notes writing is a reasonable investment; spending your whole day there steals time from the actual product, the newsletter itself.

One thing the math above misses: the Notes feed stays opaque, so you can't know for certain why one specific Note spread and another didn't. That uncertainty makes it more reliable to track a weekly average — restacks and new free subscribers — than to overreact to any single post. A one-week dip shouldn't trigger a strategy change; the real signal is the four-to-six-week trend.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What kind of content actually spreads on Substack Notes?

A short take, a concrete insight, or a behind-the-scenes note spreads the furthest, because the feed deprioritizes bare link drops as "exit" behavior. A single-idea Note that reads fine with zero added context performs the best.

### How do restacks affect the Notes algorithm?

When a writer restacks someone else's Note, that content gets shown to the restacker's own followers, which creates a signal of audience overlap. Substack doesn't publish the exact weighting, but the anecdotal evidence for restacks mattering to discoverability is consistent across writers.

### How many Notes a week is enough for a solo writer?

Four to seven Notes a week, each written in a short session around a single idea, is a sustainable cadence. Posting three or more Notes during a launch week is reported to lift total subscriber growth, though that figure hasn't been independently verified.

### How do you improve conversion from Notes to newsletter subscribers?

Conversion improves when the newsletter invitation reads as a natural follow-up to a valuable Note, rather than a link attached to every post — the always-visible "Subscribe" button on your profile already does most of that work. Pitching the paid tier before free value is proven lowers conversion instead of raising it.
