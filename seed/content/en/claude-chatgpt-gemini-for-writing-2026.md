Short answer: there's no single winner. Claude leads on long-form structure and tone control, ChatGPT wins on fast, iterative drafts with memory across sessions, and Gemini pulls ahead when your writing is grounded in an existing Google Doc or Sheet — the right tool changes with the task, not the brand.

This comparison looks at five things writers actually do: first drafts, line editing, tone/style retention, book-length context, and privacy for client work. Unlike our [deep research face-off between ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude](/en/posts/deep-research-chatgpt-gemini-claude-2026), the focus here isn't source-gathering — it's the writing itself.

## Which Is Fastest for a First Draft?

ChatGPT is the lowest-friction tool for turning a short prompt into a usable first draft; chat history and memory mean it carries over last session's tone without you repeating "remember my voice." Claude tends to produce a draft that needs fewer structural fixes, because it processes a long brief in one pass and keeps paragraph-to-paragraph transitions more consistent. Gemini pulls ahead when you're starting from an existing Google Doc or Sheet instead of a blank page — for a free-form first draft with no source document, it's usually the furthest behind of the three.

## Which Keeps Your Voice Instead of Flattening It?

All three tend to generalize a writer's distinctive voice over a long, multi-section piece — the "AI flattening" complaint is real, but it's mostly a process problem, not a hard model limit. Giving Claude an explicit style guide and "match this sentence" instruction keeps tone from drifting into the model's default polite-academic register; in ChatGPT, the same job falls to fixed Project instructions plus memory working together. In Gemini, tone retention depends on a Gem's saved instruction set, and that feature itself is mid-transition right now (more below), making it the most fragile of the three options.

## Is the Context Window Big Enough for Book-Length Work?

Yes, but the architecture differs: Anthropic's [Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2](/en/posts/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-launch), released November 9, 2026, ship with a 1-million-token context window and always-on adaptive thinking, [according to the official release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview) — enough to hold every prior chapter of a draft, your character notes, and your style guide in one conversation. ChatGPT Projects use a single shared context pool across instructions, file snippets, and chat turns; as that pool fills, older turns can get silently pruned. Gemini's paid tiers are reported, by independent sources rather than one official figure, to approach roughly 1 million tokens — and that pool is shared with a Gem's own saved instructions.

## Which Holds a Persistent Style Guide Better?

ChatGPT's Projects feature is the most predictable option here: files and instructions attached to a project don't leak outside it, and project instructions override your global custom instructions only while you're inside that project, [per OpenAI's help documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8590148-memory-and-projects) — which makes it easy to keep separate style guides per client. Claude's Projects do a similar job, and the 1-million-token window means you don't need to re-paste the whole guide every message. Gemini's persistent-instruction layer has been called a "Gem," but [Google's transition page](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/18560919) states that Gem support is being removed on different timelines by account type, with November 2026 listed for personal Google accounts — meaning a style-guide Gem is being rebuilt as a "skill" right around when this article is published.

## Does Privacy Differ for Client Work?

Yes, and the gap is widening: Anthropic's Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 require 30-day data retention per the release notes and aren't available under zero-data-retention terms unless Anthropic specifically authorizes it — worth checking against your data-processing addendum before pasting a confidential client brief into either model. In ChatGPT, memory stays in play for future responses until you delete it, so it's safer to put client-specific detail in project-scoped memory rather than general memory. Gemini's enterprise tiers carry separate data-handling terms, but that guarantee doesn't automatically extend to content pasted into a personal Gem.

## Which Tool Fits Which Writing Task?

You don't have to commit to one assistant: using ChatGPT for short-form content and quick replies, Claude for a report or book chapter that needs structural consistency, and Gemini for edits grounded in an existing Google Doc is, in practice, the lowest-friction split. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which AI assistant has the biggest context window for writing a book?

As of November 2026, Claude's Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 models offer a 1-million-token context window, according to Anthropic's official release notes — the clearest published figure of the three for carrying every prior chapter plus a style guide in one conversation. Gemini's top tiers approach a similar ceiling, but Google hasn't confirmed a separate official number for Gems specifically.

### What's the real difference between ChatGPT and Claude for keeping a consistent writing tone?

ChatGPT uses memory and Projects together to recall your tone across sessions, while Claude relies on its long context window to hold an entire style guide without resending it every message. Both still drift toward a default, slightly formal register without an explicit style instruction.

### Is Gemini getting rid of Gems?

Partly, yes: according to Google's transition page, Gem support is being phased out on different schedules by account type, with personal Google accounts affected starting November 2026, replaced by a new file-upload-based structure called "skills." Anyone with a style guide saved in an existing Gem should plan to migrate it to the skill format.

### Which assistant is safest for client writing work?

None of the three is "safest" by default — safety depends on which data-retention and project-isolation settings you turn on. Claude's newest models require 30-day data retention, ChatGPT separates project-scoped memory from general memory, and Gemini's enterprise tier carries different terms than personal use — check the relevant setting in all three before pasting client information.
