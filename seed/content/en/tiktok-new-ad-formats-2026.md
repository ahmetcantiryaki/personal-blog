Short answer: TopReach bundles TikTok's two premium placements, TopView and TopFeed, into one buy for maximum one-day reach; Prime Time runs three sequential ads from one advertiser in a 15-minute window; and Pulse Mentions places ads next to content where people are already talking about your category. TikTok introduced all four at its 2026 NewFronts event, and each targets a different budget and goal.

## What is TikTok TopReach?

TopReach combines TopView, the full-screen ad that fires the instant someone opens TikTok, with TopFeed, the first ad placement in the For You feed, into a single bundled buy. Instead of running two separate premium placements and hoping for overlap, an advertiser books both under one line item for a defined 24-hour window. TikTok positions this for launch days and flash-sale moments where the goal is reaching as much of the daily active audience as possible, not optimizing cost-per-click.

## What is Prime Time, and how is it different from a normal ad slot?

Prime Time serves the same user three sequential ads from one advertiser inside a 15-minute window, timed around a tentpole moment — a live event, a sports final, an awards show. The logic is repetition inside a high-attention window rather than one-off impressions spread across a day. TechCrunch's own reporting on the format flagged the obvious risk: [three ads from the same brand in 15 minutes](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/03/24/tiktok-ads-are-about-to-get-a-tad-more-disruptive/) can read as aggressive to users who weren't expecting a takeover, so it works best tied to a genuine cultural moment, not as a default buy.

## What does Logo Takeover do?

Logo Takeover lets a brand co-brand TikTok itself at app launch — an exclusive placement the moment a user opens the app, carrying the brand's identity alongside TikTok's. Warner Bros. used it to promote a film release and reported double-digit lifts in brand awareness and intent, though that figure comes from TikTok's own measurement, not an independent audit.

## What is Pulse Mentions, and how does it differ from Pulse Tastemakers?

Pulse Mentions places ads adjacent to organic content where creators and users are already discussing your brand or product category — riding existing conversation instead of buying space next to generic trending video. Pulse Tastemakers, the companion product in the same Pulse suite, instead aligns ads with a curated group of eligible creators regardless of what they're currently posting about. Mentions targets a conversation; Tastemakers targets a creator roster.

## Which format fits which budget and objective?

The four formats sit at very different price and reach points, so matching the format to the actual goal — not the hype around the name — decides whether the spend pays off.

| Format | Best for | Budget tier | What it optimizes |
|---|---|---|---|
| TopReach | Product launches, flash sales | High (premium placement bundle) | One-day reach across the daily active audience |
| Prime Time | Tentpole/live-event campaigns | High, event-gated | Repetition inside a short high-attention window |
| Logo Takeover | Brand/franchise launches | High | App-open brand awareness |
| Pulse Mentions | Category or branded conversation | Mid | Relevance by proximity to existing chatter |
| Pulse Tastemakers | Creator-aligned brand campaigns | Mid | Relevance by curated creator roster |

Small advertisers running under roughly $10K/month should skip TopReach, Prime Time, and Logo Takeover entirely — all three are built around premium, largely non-negotiable placement fees that only make sense when reach itself is the KPI. Pulse Mentions and Pulse Tastemakers are the more realistic entry points for a mid-sized budget, since both ride existing organic placement logic rather than buying an exclusive slot.

## How do you measure these formats without over-crediting last click?

Reach-oriented formats like TopReach and Prime Time rarely show up as the last click before a conversion — their job is awareness, not direct response, so judging them on last-click conversions will always make them look like a waste of budget. Run a holdout or geo-split test instead: expose one audience segment to the format and withhold it from a matched segment, then compare conversion rates between the two groups over the following one to two weeks. That isolates the incremental lift the format actually drove, separate from whatever would have converted anyway.

```text
Baseline segment: no TopReach exposure, standard feed ads only
Test segment: TopReach exposure during launch window
Compare: conversion rate, branded search volume, 7-day and 14-day lift
```

## How do you decide which format to actually buy?

Start from the objective, not the format name. If the goal is pure one-day reach around a specific date, TopReach or Logo Takeover are the only formats built for that. If the goal is riding an event your audience already cares about, Prime Time only makes sense when that event is real and relevant to your category — buying it around a generic moment wastes the premium. If the goal is relevance and organic-feeling presence on a mid-sized budget, Pulse Mentions and Pulse Tastemakers do that job without the premium placement fee. One more filter worth applying before any of these: ask whether the campaign has a hard deadline. TopReach, Prime Time, and Logo Takeover are all built around a fixed window, so they only make sense when there's an actual date — a launch, a drop, a live event — driving the spend; without that date, the same budget works harder spread across ongoing Pulse-tier placements. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the difference between TikTok TopReach and TopView?

TopView is a single premium placement — the full-screen ad on app open. TopReach bundles TopView together with TopFeed, the first in-feed ad, into one buy for maximum reach across a defined 24-hour window.

### Is TikTok Prime Time worth it for a small brand?

Rarely. Prime Time is priced for tentpole moments and stacks three ads from one brand in 15 minutes, which only reads as relevant rather than aggressive when tied to a real event your audience cares about. Most small advertisers get better value from Pulse Mentions.

### How is Pulse Mentions different from a regular TikTok ad?

A regular TikTok ad buys placement in the feed based on targeting rules. Pulse Mentions specifically places your ad next to organic content where people are already discussing your brand or category, riding existing conversation instead of competing for generic attention.

### How do you measure the incremental impact of a reach-focused format like TopReach?

Run a holdout test: expose one audience segment and withhold a matched segment, then compare conversion rates and branded search volume between the two over the following one to two weeks. Last-click attribution alone will undercount a reach format's real impact.
