Short answer: TikTok's Local Feed is a dedicated tab that surfaces nearby business, place, and event content, ranked by proximity, topic, and recency rather than the interest graph that drives the main For You feed. TikTok launched it in the US in February 2026, after rolling out a European version called "Nearby" in December 2025.

## What is TikTok's Local Feed and how does its ranking work?

TikTok's own announcement describes the Local Feed as a tab that sits alongside For You and Following, showing only public content from accounts near the viewer's current location. Its ranking does not run on the same interest-graph signals that decide what shows up on the main feed; instead, it weighs location proximity, content topic, and how recently a post went up.

That distinction matters for a small shop. Winning on the main feed usually means competing for broad attention across millions of accounts. Winning on the Local Feed means competing against the handful of other businesses posting in the same neighborhood or city district. The European "Nearby" version launched in France, Germany, Italy, and the UK in December 2025, and the US "Local Feed" expanded the same core mechanic in February 2026 under a different name.

One honest caveat: this is not a global feature yet. As of October 2026, TikTok has not announced a rollout date for markets outside these regions. A shop owner anywhere else should build habits around place tags and local hashtags that work regardless of whether the tab exists in their country, rather than betting the whole strategy on a feed that may not appear for months.

## How do you turn on location sharing, and what's the privacy trade-off?

To see or appear in the Local Feed, a user has to turn on precise location sharing in their device settings; the setting is off by default, and TikTok states it is available only to users 18 and older. Accounts belonging to users under 18 are also excluded from appearing in the Local or Nearby feed.

The privacy wrinkle worth knowing: some reporting notes that TikTok may continue collecting precise location data even when the Local Feed toggle itself is off. For a business owner testing this on a personal account, the practical takeaway is that turning on location sharing is a deliberate trade between personal privacy and local discoverability, and it can be switched off again at any time from settings.

A business account does not need to enable personal location sharing to benefit. Adding a place tag to a post is generally a stronger and more direct signal than the poster's own location setting, since it ties the content to a specific address regardless of who is viewing it. ## What content actually performs on the Local Feed?

Four content types consistently perform better in local, proximity-based discovery: place-tagged posts, event posts, behind-the-counter or day-in-the-life clips, and videos using local hashtags. Each one gives the ranking system a clean location or topic signal to key off.

| Content type | Why it works locally |
|---|---|
| Place tag | Ties the post directly to an address, raising odds of surfacing to nearby viewers |
| Event post | Strong recency signal that matches "happening this week" local intent |
| Behind-the-counter / day-in-the-life clip | Reads as real and verifiable, so viewers recognize the actual place |
| Local hashtag (#cityname, #neighborhoodname) | Reinforces the topic signal and matches local search behavior |

What these four formats share is that they feel real, nearby, and current to the viewer. Polished, studio-style ad content tends to underperform here because the system's location and topic signals read as weaker or more generic. Local Feed visibility and TikTok Shop sales are not technically integrated, but they feed the same behavior: a viewer discovers a nearby business, taps into the profile, and a visible product or service turns that curiosity into a purchase. Connecting a shop's catalog to TikTok Shop is the shortest path to converting Local Feed traffic into revenue.

In practice, the chain looks like this: a place-tagged video surfaces on the Local Feed, the viewer clicks into the profile, and the profile's storefront or bio link leads to a TikTok Shop listing. ## What's a simple weekly posting plan a solo shop owner can follow?

Posting 3–4 times a week, varying the content type and adding a place tag to every post, is a sustainable cadence for a one-person business. Daily posting is not required; consistency and format variety matter more than raw frequency.

| Day | Content type | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Behind-the-counter / prep clip | Build a sense of realness, add place tag |
| Wednesday | Product or service spotlight | Drive traffic to the TikTok Shop link |
| Friday | "Pick of the week" with a local hashtag | Catch local search intent |
| Weekend (optional) | Event or special-day post | Recency signal, time-sensitive interest |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is TikTok's Local Feed available outside the US and Europe?

As of October 2026, TikTok has only confirmed the Local Feed in the US (launched February 2026) and the "Nearby" version in France, Germany, Italy, and the UK (launched December 2025). No other market has an announced rollout date, so a shop outside these regions should build its strategy around place tags and local hashtags rather than the tab itself.

### What's the minimum age for TikTok's location-sharing feature?

TikTok states that precise location sharing is available only to users 18 and older, and it is off by default. Accounts belonging to users under 18 are also not eligible to appear in the Local or Nearby feed, regardless of whether the viewer has location sharing turned on.

### Do you need to turn on location sharing to show up in the Local Feed?

No, it is not required. Adding a place tag to a post is a stronger, more direct signal than the poster's own location setting, since it ties the content to a specific address independent of the viewer's personal location permissions.

### How many posts per week are enough for Local Feed visibility?

Posting 3 to 4 times a week, rotating through behind-the-counter clips, product spotlights, event posts, and local-hashtag content, is a sustainable and effective cadence for a one-person business. Posting more often can help, but frequency alone does not substitute for format variety.
