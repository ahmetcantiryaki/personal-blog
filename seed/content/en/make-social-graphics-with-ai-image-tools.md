Short answer: pick GPT Image 2 when your graphic needs readable text, ordered panels, or exact layout — think carousels — and pick Nano Banana 2 when it needs photorealism, texture, or a camera-shot feel, like a product hero. Mixing the two beats picking just one, since each is genuinely stronger at a different job.

## Which AI image tool should you pick for social graphics?

If the graphic needs readable text, sequential panels, or precise placement, go with GPT Image 2 — it leads in text-rendering reliability, structural control, and complex multi-element compositions. If it needs photorealism, skin or material texture, or a hero shot that feels camera-captured, Nano Banana 2 does better; released in February 2026, it combines the strength of Nano Banana Pro with Gemini Flash's speed, generates at 4K, and can pull from Gemini's real-time web knowledge to render specific subjects more accurately.

A third option, dedicated design AI (Canva's Magic Studio, for example), is good for fast template-based output, but keeping your brand color and font consistent usually means preloading a brand kit first.

## Which tool works best for carousels, thumbnails, and quote cards?

| Content type | Recommended tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Text-heavy carousel | GPT Image 2 | Reliable text rendering, ordered panel layout |
| Product/hero thumbnail | Nano Banana 2 | Photorealism, cinematic lighting |
| Quote card | GPT Image 2 | Precise text placement, legibility |
| Background/mood visual | Nano Banana 2 | Fast visual iteration, texture quality |

Treat this table as a starting point, not a hard rule. If a carousel's cover slide needs a photorealistic base image with a small amount of text on top, generating the base with Nano Banana 2 first and adding the text as a separate layer produces a more reliable result than asking GPT Image 2 to do both in one pass.

## What's the right size and format for a carousel or thumbnail?

Aspect ratio varies by platform, and a graphic generated at the wrong ratio gets cropped and looks broken no matter how good the AI output is. Specifying the size directly in the prompt is far more reliable than trying to crop it afterward.

| Platform / format | Recommended aspect ratio |
|---|---|
| Instagram feed carousel | 4:5 (vertical) |
| Instagram/Facebook Stories | 9:16 |
| LinkedIn post image | 1:1 or 4:5 |
| YouTube thumbnail | 16:9 |

Generating every slide of a carousel at the same aspect ratio and the same margins eliminates the visual "jump" that shows up when slides don't quite match as a viewer swipes through. Adding a fixed resolution and ratio instruction to your prompt template (for example, "1080x1350px, 4:5 ratio") makes that consistency automatic instead of something you check by eye.

## How do you write prompts that keep your brand consistent?

Open every prompt with a fixed "style block": your brand hex codes, preferred lighting (soft vs. hard), composition preference (centered vs. rule-of-thirds), and your brand font name if you have one. Storing that block in a text file and pasting it in each time — instead of rewriting it from memory — eliminates the most common cause of graphics that drift off-brand within a few weeks.

Uploading a reference image beats describing your brand in words alone: giving the model your best-performing past graphic and saying "this style, but for this topic" produces far more consistent output than letting it guess your identity from text.

## How do you get in-image text right reliably?

Work in two passes instead of one: generate the image without text first, then run a second pass on the same model asking it to add specific text to a specific region. GPT Image 2 is more reliable for complex, multi-element layouts and exact placement; Nano Banana 2 is good enough for simpler needs like a marketing mock-up or a greeting card, and can even translate and localize in-image text into another language.

Don't assume either model renders long text blocks reliably — anything past a few words. Eyeball every text block by hand after generation, every time.

## How do you batch a week of social graphics?

At the start of the week, collect every piece of text you'll need across the week's posts — carousel headlines, quotes, thumbnail captions — into one list, then generate all of them in the same session with the same style block. That beats writing a separate prompt and re-opening the tool for each post individually; graphics generated in one sitting read as more visually consistent than ones produced across scattered days.

Keep everything you generate in an "image bank" folder, even what you don't use that week. Reusing a theme the following month means pulling from the bank instead of starting from zero.

If you want a template system, using AI purely for backgrounds and mood visuals, then dropping text layers into a fixed template in a tool like Canva, is a good middle ground between speed and consistency. That separates what should vary (the AI-generated image) from what needs to stay fixed (font, logo placement, brand color), which produces fewer mistakes than trying to cram both into a single AI prompt.

## What about licensing and disclosure for AI-generated graphics?

Check the terms of service for whichever tool you use: both OpenAI and Google allow commercial use, but generating an exact likeness of a real brand's logo, a celebrity's face, or a copyrighted character can violate either platform's policy. Never publish a generated image resembling a real, identifiable person without that person's consent.

Disclosure rules vary by platform — some ad networks require labeling AI-generated visuals. When in doubt, a small "Made with AI" note in the corner costs far less than fielding a transparency question after the fact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which AI image tool is best for a social media carousel?

GPT Image 2 is more reliable for text-heavy, sequential carousels because it leads in text rendering and structural control. If you want a photorealistic cover image, generate the base with Nano Banana 2 first and add text as a separate layer for a better result.

### What's the difference between Nano Banana 2 and GPT Image 2?

Nano Banana 2 leads in photorealism, cinematic lighting, and fast visual iteration. GPT Image 2 is stronger on text-rendering reliability and complex multi-element compositions. The choice comes down to whether the graphic needs text precision or photorealism.

### Can I use AI-generated images commercially?

Generally yes — both OpenAI and Google allow commercial use of images made with their tools. But generating an exact likeness of a real brand's logo, a celebrity's face, or a copyrighted character can violate platform policy or copyright, so avoid that kind of content.

### How do you keep AI-generated graphics on-brand?

Open every prompt with a fixed style block covering your brand colors, lighting preference, and composition style, and upload your best-performing past graphic as a reference. That produces far more consistent output than letting the model guess your brand identity from text alone.
