Maria sells hand-thrown ceramic mugs on Instagram. A commenter asks how she gets the glaze so even; she replies publicly, then moves to DM with a process photo, and three messages later places an order. Short answer: turning followers into customers is a repeatable sequence — a comment becomes a DM, the DM earns trust, and trust unlocks a low-pressure offer.

None of that requires an ad budget, just the right question at the right moment. As of August 2026, that sequence matters more than ever, since discovery has moved off search and onto social.

## Why does social media now beat search for product discovery?

Short answer: YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram combined now drive over 60% of product discovery, while Google holds roughly 34.5% of total search share. "People Google it first" no longer holds for most buyers.

The shift is sharper among younger buyers: 46% of Gen Z now start shopping on social media, overtaking Google Search as the primary discovery channel for that age group for the first time on record, and TikTok alone captures roughly 29% of Gen Z product searches globally. Your audience is already deciding what to buy in your comments and DMs — join that conversation, or someone else's product will.

The global social commerce market is worth roughly $2.11 trillion in 2026 at a 29.12% CAGR; US social commerce sales cross roughly $100.99 billion this year, up about 18% year over year — growth that flows partly through exchanges as small as Maria's.

## What's the difference between content that attracts fans and content that attracts buyers?

Short answer: content built for fans entertains; content built for buyers shows a real decision point and leaves the solution in your hands — one collects likes, the other collects questions.

Maria's highest-converting posts aren't her most-liked ones: finished-mug photos rack up likes, but "how I fixed this cracked handle" videos rack up comments that move to DMs. Buyer-attracting content shows a genuine decision and a comment-worthy detail.

82% of consumers use social platforms to discover and research products; your content's job is turning that research into a conversation, not just a view count. ## How do you turn a comment into a DM conversation?

Short answer: reply publicly first, then use a natural bridge line — "want me to send the details over DM?" — to move the conversation private. Your first DM should never be a sales pitch.

The order matters: a public reply shows a real person runs the account; DM lets you share specifics — price, stock, a custom option — you wouldn't post openly. Maria's rule: her first three DM messages never mention selling, only the question asked. By message four, the other person has usually asked how to order.

A common mistake: sellers open a DM with a price list before earning a reply — the one I see most often. Wait until the conversation is genuinely two-sided.

## How do lead magnets and soft CTAs work on social media?

Short answer: a social lead magnet doesn't need an email form — a story-highlight tip, a bio-linked guide, or a DM discount code works just as well, by inviting a next step rather than forcing a purchase.

A soft CTA replaces "buy now" with something lower-pressure, like "check the link in my bio." Maria keeps a "mug care guide" linked there; most people who click it send a follow-up question within a week — not a sale, but it warms up the next contact.

## How do you build trust before you pitch?

Short answer: trust builds through consistent small interactions — timely comment replies, honest answers, and regular non-sales content. The pitch should feel like a natural result of that pattern, not the first thing someone hears from you.

Maria doesn't pitch every new follower; she posts process videos, honest mistakes, and open answers to questions — the social version of "give before you ask." The signal you're ready to pitch: someone has asked a second or third question, since pitching right after the first is usually too early.

## How do you use AI to personalize small-scale DM outreach?

Short answer: AI produces a draft instead of a blank page, which you edit against the person's question and your usual tone — a faster starting point, not automated bulk messaging.

When Maria gets dozens of similar questions daily, she has AI draft the typical answer, then edits in the person's name and the detail from their message — different from bulk outreach, since the reply reads as written for them. AI should never handle first contact; save it for repetitive follow-ups only.

## How do you build a simple funnel from profile to purchase?

Short answer: the funnel has four steps — a post that earns attention, a clear next step in your bio, a real DM conversation, and an easy, obvious way to buy. Each step should leave the person more committed than the last.

| Funnel step | Social-media equivalent | How it works in Maria's example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | Process video or a post showing a decision point | "How I get this glaze color" video |
| First contact | Natural bridge from comment to DM | "Want me to send details over DM?" |
| Trust | Real, non-sales answers in DM | Photo of the glazing process, material details |
| Purchase | Bio link or a simple order step in DM | "Just tap the link in my bio to order" |

## Which platform fits which kind of seller?

Short answer: platform choice depends on price point and audience age — conversion rates differ meaningfully across platforms, so putting everything into one is a risk.

| Platform | Typical conversion rate | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok Shop | ~3.8% | Visual, fast-decision products; younger audiences |
| Facebook Shop | ~1.2% | Older audiences, more considered purchases |
| Instagram (DM/bio-led) | Not separately reported; strong on high-engagement accounts | Handmade, custom, or service-based products |

TikTok Shop's conversion rate is more than triple Facebook Shop's, and it's projected to hit roughly $23.41 billion in US sales in 2026, up about 48% year over year. The average American social shopper spends roughly $1,274 a year in-app — per [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-commerce-statistics/).

## How do you measure conversion in social selling?

Short answer: track three numbers — commenters who move to DM, DMs that get a genuine reply, and conversations ending in a sale. Follower count isn't useful here.

Maria tracks these ratios monthly: comments-to-DMs, DMs-to-back-and-forth, conversations-to-orders. Even if her follower count stays flat, an improvement in any one ratio means the strategy works. Treating follower count as a proxy for sales is social selling's most time-wasting metric mistake.

## DM script and profile-to-sale checklist

```text
[Public comment reply]
Hi! I use a two-coat glaze technique to get that
tone — want me to walk you through it over DM?

[DM message 1 - genuine answer, no pitch]
Hey, thanks for asking! I apply a white base coat
first, then a cobalt glaze on the second firing.
I'll send a photo of the process.

[DM message 2 - if they ask a follow-up]
This size holds about 10 oz, good for daily use.
The smaller one is closer to 8 oz and looks nicer
as a gift. Which one fits what you have in mind?

[DM message 3 - once interest is clear]
You can order through the link in my bio, or just
tell me the color and size here and I'll set it up
for you — whichever is easier.
```

- Does your bio have one clear next step (a link, a DM invite, or a guide)?
- Do at least half your last ten posts show a decision point or process, not just a finished product?
- Is your average comment reply time under a few hours?
- Are your first three DM messages free of any sales pitch?
- Are you tracking comment-to-DM-to-sale ratios monthly?
- Is AI limited to repeat-question drafts, never first contact?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many followers do I need to start social selling?

There's no fixed threshold; a few hundred active followers is enough, since social selling runs on conversation quality, not volume. A small but engaged following converts into more sales than a much larger, silent one.

### Is it wrong to mention price in the first DM?

Answering if they ask isn't wrong, but opening with a price pitch usually lands too early. Answer their actual question first — price and ordering should come naturally by the third or fourth message.

### Does using AI make my DMs feel impersonal?

Only if you send an unedited, generic draft. Editing it to the person's name and question, and writing first contact yourself, keeps it personal — the tool isn't the problem, sending it unedited is.
