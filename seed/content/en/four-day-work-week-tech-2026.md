Short answer: yes, with one condition — weekly hours have to actually drop, not just get squeezed into four days instead of five. The largest controlled study to date, published in 2025, tracked 141 companies and 2,896 workers across six countries; about 90% of participating firms kept the shortened week after six months, and self-reported burnout measurably fell.

That finding isn't a one-off. From the 2022 UK pilot to a 2026 Australian trial, multiple independent studies point the same direction. But "four-day week" covers two very different arrangements, and conflating them is where most casual coverage goes wrong.

## What's the difference between a four-day week and a compressed week?

A true four-day week cuts total working hours — typically from 40 down to 32. A compressed week spreads the same 40 hours over four longer days instead of five. The two get mixed up constantly in media coverage, but their effects diverge sharply: when days drop without hours actually falling, the burnout benefit mostly disappears, because the employee's total workload hasn't changed.

Almost all the studies below test the real-hours-reduction model, with pay held constant. That's why the Australian trial names its model "100:80:100": 100% of pay, 80% of hours, 100% of expected output.

## What did the largest 2025 controlled study find?

The 2025 study published in Nature Human Behaviour is the most comprehensive controlled trial on this topic to date. It followed 141 companies and 2,896 workers across six countries for six months on a shortened schedule. The result: roughly 90% of participating companies chose to keep running the four-day week after the trial ended, and workers reported both lower burnout and higher job satisfaction. The sample is large, but participating companies opted in voluntarily — so the results don't automatically generalize to every industry or company size.

## Is the 2022 UK pilot still holding up a year later?

Yes — the most frequently cited pilot is still standing. According to [follow-up data compiled by Success.com](https://www.success.com/4-day-work-week-research-2026), of the 61 companies that joined the 2022 UK pilot, at least 54 reported still running a four-day week in the one-year follow-up. That's a sign companies adopted the change as lasting policy rather than a temporary PR move. The pilot's own first six months had shown a similar retention rate, with roughly 90% of participating companies continuing.

## What did Australia's "100:80:100" model teach us?

The Australian study, published in Nature in June 2026 and covered by [Allwork.space](https://allwork.space/2026/06/four-day-workweek-delivers-full-productivity-with-fewer-hours-australian-study-shows/), followed 15 companies running the "100:80:100" model: employees kept 100% of their pay while working 80% of their hours, and companies expected 100% of prior output. The result was clear — reported burnout fell 64% and stress dropped 38%, with no measured drop in output. It's one of the most current data-backed answers to "doesn't cutting hours mean work doesn't get done?"

## How do tech companies actually run this in practice?

[Analytics Insight's survey of the sector](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/jobs/four-day-workweek-tech-jobs-companies-roles-to-watch-in-2026) names Buffer and Wildbit as two well-known tech companies that have run a four-day week as permanent policy for years. Both operate with fully remote teams, which forces them to also cut down on meeting load and synchronous communication when they shorten the week. As we cover in [how remote developers grow their careers](/en/posts/remote-developer-career-growth), remote teams already lean on asynchronous communication by default — shortening the week just makes that structure non-negotiable.

## What's the most common mistake teams make shifting to a four-day week?

The most common mistake is cutting the calendar without cutting the workload first. If a company simply compresses five days of work into four without changing anything else, employees try to finish the same meeting load, the same deadlines, and the same inbox volume in less time — and burnout doesn't fall, it can spike in the short term. What the UK pilot companies had in common was cutting meeting counts and removing low-priority work from the list before the schedule changed, not just moving the calendar around.

The second common mistake is running the pilot too short. It took six months for companies in the Nature study to see a measurable result; a trial of a few weeks isn't long enough for teams to drop old habits like last-minute meetings or "quick" Friday-afternoon syncs. The third mistake is skipping measurement entirely: trying to answer "did it work?" without retention data or burnout surveys means deciding on impression rather than evidence.

## Does this work for every team?

My honest take: the data is real, but it isn't universal. These studies draw on small, self-selected samples of companies that volunteered, which doesn't prove the result transfers to every team size or sector. Shift-based support teams, roles requiring constant customer contact, or jobs tied to tight regulatory calendars have a much harder time running a genuine four-day week. As we've argued in [how to avoid burnout as a developer](/en/posts/avoid-developer-burnout), the real problem is usually how work gets distributed, not the raw hour count — and a four-day week forces that distribution to get rethought, which is exactly why it works.

Software teams get an extra advantage here: as we cover in [does AI actually make developers faster](/en/posts/does-ai-make-developers-faster), the real bottleneck for most teams isn't typing speed, it's meetings and context switching. A four-day week attacks that bottleneck directly, which is a reasonable explanation for why the results land more consistently in software than in sectors built around constant availability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a four-day week the same as a compressed week?

No. A four-day week genuinely cuts total hours (for example, from 40 to 32), while a compressed week spreads the same total hours over four longer days instead of five. The positive results in these studies only show up under real hour reduction.

### Does pay drop when a company moves to a four-day week?

Not in the model these studies measure. The 2025 Nature study and Australia's 100:80:100 trial both kept employee pay at 100% while reducing hours, with companies still expecting the same output.

### How does a small software team move to a four-day week?

The most common path is a four-to-six-month pilot period, cutting meeting load, and prioritizing asynchronous communication. Most companies in the UK pilot followed this approach and were still running the schedule a year later.

### Which tech companies have made the four-day week permanent?

Buffer and Wildbit are among the best-known tech companies that have sustained a four-day week for years. Both run fully remote teams and redesigned their communication structure around the shorter week rather than just removing a day from the calendar.
