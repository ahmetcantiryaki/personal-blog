According to [SparkToro's 2026 data](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/), fewer than a third of Google searches now result in a click to any site — the share of searches generating a click has fallen 9.5 percentage points (a 22.9% decline) since 2024. In this environment, writing standalone, context-free blog posts works less and less. The winning strategy is building interlinked content clusters that cover a topic end to end.

## What Topical Authority Is and Why AI Search Rewards It

Topical authority means becoming the most trusted, most complete source on a subject — not one comprehensive article, but a network of interlinked content covering every angle of that topic. Per [getpassionfruit.com's analysis](https://www.getpassionfruit.com/blog/topical-authority-clusters-for-ai-search-citations), domains with 10 or more interlinked pages on a topic earn AI citations at 2 to 3 times the rate of sites competing with single pages. The reason is simple: when an LLM answers a question, it trusts a source that demonstrates depth on a topic, not just a single surface-level mention.

## The Real Impact of Google AI Overviews

The numbers paint a clear picture: AI Overviews now appear on more than 20% of Google searches, with query-level presence reaching roughly 48% by April 2026. When an AI Overview appears, organic click-through rate drops by nearly 60%.

| Metric | Value |

This table can read like a threat, but it's also an opportunity: as clicks shrink, *which* site gets referenced matters even more — and that choice isn't random, it rests on measurable signals.

## Pillar Pages and Cluster Mapping

A topic cluster consists of one pillar page and 5 to 30 supporting articles. The pillar page frames the topic broadly and links out to every subtopic; the supporting articles each dig deep into their subtopic and link back to the pillar. For example, if "API design" is your pillar, articles like "REST vs GraphQL," "API versioning strategies," and "idempotent API design" form its cluster — [exactly what we've done on this site](/en/posts/rest-vs-graphql).

## Internal Linking and Information Gain

For cluster mapping to actually work, two elements have to work together: structural internal linking and original information. A hub-and-spoke internal linking structure — the pillar linking to every supporting article, and every supporting article linking back to the pillar — pushes AI citation rates from around 12% to 41% on pillar-topic queries. On top of that, articles cited in AI Overviews cover 62% more facts on average than uncited articles on the same topic. The takeaway: an article that just summarizes other sources loses; one carrying original data, your own measurements, or a genuinely distinct perspective wins.

## How to Measure Authority

There are three practical ways to track topical authority: (1) the average ranking position across pages in the target cluster — the cluster as a whole, not a single page's rank; (2) citation frequency in AI search tools (ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews), which you can track weekly with manual test queries; (3) the cluster's total share of organic traffic, not individual pages'. Sites that build clusters correctly see an average 40% increase in organic traffic compared to sites without a clustered content strategy.

## The Build Sequence for a Small Site

If you're starting from zero, the order matters: pick the single highest-commercial-value topic first, write its pillar page, then break the pillar's subtopics into 8–12 supporting articles, linking each back to the pillar as it publishes. Don't start a second topic before finishing the first one — three half-finished clusters generate fewer AI citations than one complete cluster, because none of them clears the "comprehensive" bar.

## How Often to Refresh Pillar Pages

Publishing a cluster isn't the finish line — the pillar page, being the most-trafficked and most-linked piece of the cluster, is also the part that goes stale fastest. A practical rule: review pillar pages quarterly (update statistics, fix broken outbound links, link in new supporting articles), and only update supporting articles when something concrete about the subtopic actually changes. Once a pillar page's "last updated" date crosses six months, both search engines and AI models start treating it as stale — and that drop is sharper in fast-moving topics like SEO or AI tooling.

## Cluster-Planning Template

1. Pick your highest-commercial-value topic and write the pillar page.
2. Break the pillar into 8–12 subtopics, each as its own article.
3. Include at least one original data point or measurement in every supporting article.
4. Implement hub-and-spoke internal linking: bidirectional links between pillar and every supporting article.
5. Don't start a new topic before the current cluster is fully published.
6. Track AI search citation frequency weekly with manual test queries.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many articles should a topic cluster include?

The general rule is one pillar page plus 5 to 30 supporting articles. For a small site, a cluster of 8–12 articles is usually manageable while still clearing the "comprehensive" bar.

### Why is hub-and-spoke internal linking so effective?

It clearly signals to search engines and AI models which pages relate to each other and which page is the topic's central source. Measured data shows this structure can push AI citation rates from 12% to 41%.

### Does the AI Overviews click decline apply to small sites too?

Yes, the decline reflects a broad shift in search behavior independent of site size. But the citation advantage is equally open to small sites — what determines it is authority, not brand size.

### Is sharing original data mandatory?

Not mandatory, but a strong advantage. Articles cited in AI Overviews carry 62% more facts on average — evidence that sharing your own measurements or experience pays off more than summarizing other sources.
