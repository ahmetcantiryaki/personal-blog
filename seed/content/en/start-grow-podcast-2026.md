Short answer: starting a podcast in 2026 needs one USB microphone, a quiet room softened with a rug or curtains, and an AI-assisted editing stack like Riverside or Descript. Growth comes from a schedule you keep, clipping episodes into short video, and picking guests deliberately — not from gear. As of August 2026, inconsistency is the biggest blocker for new shows.

## Which format and niche should you pick?

Your format depends on how much time you have and who you can realistically book as a guest. Solo commentary, two-host conversation, and guest interviews are the three most common formats, and locking episode length to 25–40 minutes makes it easier for listeners to build a habit around your show.

The niche rule is blunt: a narrow niche published consistently beats a broad niche published erratically, almost every time. "A tech podcast" loses to "a growth podcast for early-stage SaaS founders in Turkey" because a specific definition makes both guest booking and sponsor outreach easier. Once the niche is locked, planning the first ten episode titles in advance avoids the week-two problem of staring at a blank outline.

## How much gear and room setup do you actually need?

Short answer: a USB microphone in the $100–150 range (a Shure MV7 or Rode NT-USB+, for example), a decent pair of headphones, and a small room with soft surfaces — carpet, curtains, a bookshelf — is enough for most new shows. You do not need acoustic panels or a rented studio; even a closet full of clothes can produce broadcast-clean audio.

For video, skip the webcam and use an entry-level mirrorless camera or a 1080p webcam plus one soft light source. [Riverside records each participant's audio and video locally](https://riverside.com/blog/how-to-publish-a-podcast) on their own device before uploading, so a shaky internet connection during the call does not degrade the final file. That local-recording architecture alone makes it more reliable than a plain Zoom recording for shows with remote guests.

## How do AI tools handle editing, transcripts, show notes, and clips?

Short answer: as of August 2026, Descript lets you cut audio and video by editing a text transcript, Riverside turns a conversation into a transcript, draft show notes, and vertical clip suggestions automatically, and Adobe Podcast offers free AI noise removal and voice clarity for cleaning up a raw take fast.

| Tool | Strength | Starting price (Aug 2026) | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descript | Text-based editing of audio/video, filler-word removal, Studio Sound enhancement | ~$24/mo (Creator plan) | Solo editing, fast subtitled clip export |
| Riverside | Local-recording architecture, auto transcripts, AI show notes, vertical "Magic Clips" | ~$15–24/mo | Remote guest recording, clip generation |
| Adobe Podcast | Free noise removal and speech clarity (Enhance Speech) | Core tools free | Cleaning a raw take before it goes anywhere else |

Many creators run both: [record in Riverside, fine-edit in Descript](https://www.creatorstackclub.com/software/riverside-editor). Combined, the two Creator-tier plans run roughly $35–40 a month — still far cheaper than hiring an editor. ## Where should you distribute your podcast?

Short answer: upload the episode to one hosting service (Spotify for Creators, Buzzsprout, Transistor), let it generate an RSS feed, and submit that feed to Apple Podcasts and Spotify. If you record video, you upload the same episode to YouTube separately.

| Platform | US podcast-listening share (2026) | Video support | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spotify | 30%+ | Strong, via Partner Program | Monetization opens at 1,000 engaged listeners, 2,000 hours streamed in the past 30 days, and 3 published episodes |
| Apple Podcasts | ~25% | Limited, audio-first | The reference point for classic RSS-based distribution |
| YouTube | 25%+ | Primary video platform | Now the second-largest podcast-listening destination in the US |

RSS is the XML feed format that pushes your episode to every directory at once. A video-compatible episode is declared with an `enclosure` tag like this:

```xml
<item>
  <title>Episode 12: Producing a Podcast with AI Tools</title>
  <enclosure url="https://cdn.example.com/episode-12.mp4" length="52428800" type="video/mp4" />
  <itunes:duration>00:38:12</itunes:duration>
</item>
```

As of January 2026, [Spotify's Distribution API](https://thepodcastconsultant.com/blog/spotify-for-creators) works with hosts including Acast, Audioboom, Libsyn, Omny, and Podigee, and most of them generate that RSS feed for you automatically — you rarely need to hand-write the XML.

## How do you repurpose episodes into short video and posts?

Short answer: pulling 3–5 vertical clips of 30–90 seconds, one LinkedIn or X post, and a short blog recap out of every 30–40-minute episode turns a single recording into five or six separate pieces of content. Riverside's Magic Clips feature auto-detects the strongest moments, so you are mostly editing caption style and cover text rather than scrubbing through raw footage.

Captions are not optional here. ## What actually grows a podcast — loops and guest strategy?

Short answer: the most reliable growth loop is booking a guest who already has an audience, then having that guest share the episode with their own following — this is the single biggest source of organic reach for a show with no ad budget.

Three guest-strategy habits move the needle: send guests 3–4 concrete questions before recording (it sharpens the improvisation), hand them a ready-made clip-and-quote pack the day the episode goes live (it removes the friction to share), and invite strong guests back 6–12 months later (it builds a recurring listener bond). Planning the release calendar in advance matters just as much; our [batch-a-month-of-content method](/en/posts/batch-month-content-in-a-day) maps directly onto a podcast's clip-and-post calendar.

Here is the opinionated part: most new podcasts do not fail because of bad gear — they fail because the host cannot hold a same-day-every-week release habit for three months straight. No algorithm change fixes an inconsistent publishing calendar.

## How do you monetize a podcast?

Short answer: as of August 2026 there are four realistic revenue paths — platform ad-revenue sharing ([Spotify's Partner Program](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/business/spotify-partner-program-podcasts-revenue-creators/)), direct sponsor deals, listener-paid subscriptions (Apple Podcasts Subscriptions, Patreon), and in-episode promotion of your own product or service. Under the Spotify Partner Program, Spotify keeps roughly half of ad revenue generated through the program, with the rest coming from both Premium video streams and ad-supported views.

A realistic sequence for a new show: promote your own product or service in-episode before chasing sponsors, apply for platform ad-sharing once you clear 1,000 engaged listeners, and start direct sponsor conversations once downloads pass roughly 5,000 per episode. Paid subscriptions usually convert only the top 2–5% of the most loyal audience, so treat them as supplemental revenue rather than the primary plan.

## What should your launch checklist and repurposing map look like?

Completing these eight items before episode one goes live prevents most of the chaos that hits new shows in month one:

1. Write the niche and target-listener definition in one sentence.
2. Test-record the microphone, headphones, and room to confirm the audio is clean.
3. Pick a hosting service and generate the RSS feed.
4. Pre-create the show on Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and YouTube.
5. Plan the first ten episode titles and a rough guest list.
6. Lock the record-and-edit workflow (e.g., Riverside plus Descript).
7. Finalize cover art, an intro/outro script, and a release calendar.
8. Stockpile the first three episodes before publishing episode one.

The per-episode repurposing map works like this: a single recording becomes a blog recap pulled from the transcript, 3–5 vertical clips selected from that same transcript, a LinkedIn/X post built from clip quotes, and a newsletter drawn from the show notes. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to start a podcast?

A basic setup — USB microphone, headphones, and a free or entry-level editing tool — can be assembled for $100–200. Adding video means budgeting another $150–300 for a camera and a light; a rented studio or acoustic paneling is not required to sound professional.

### Which microphone is good enough for beginners?

A USB, cardioid-pattern microphone like the Shure MV7 or Rode NT-USB+ delivers broadcast-quality audio in most home rooms. An XLR microphone and separate audio interface only become necessary once you are recording multiple people in the same room or targeting a fully professional studio sound.

### Which platform should you publish to first?

Upload the episode to a single hosting service and let it push the RSS feed to Apple Podcasts and Spotify; if you have a video recording, upload that same episode to YouTube separately. As of August 2026, Spotify and YouTube together account for more than half of US podcast listening, so neither can be skipped.

### When does a podcast start making money?

For most shows, a realistic threshold is reaching 1,000 engaged listeners 3–6 months after starting a consistent schedule — close to the minimum needed to apply for platform ad-sharing programs like Spotify's Partner Program. Sponsorship revenue typically becomes meaningful only after downloads pass roughly 5,000 per episode.
